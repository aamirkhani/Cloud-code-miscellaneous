# Manager-Run Routes Anchor Dublin's Top 100

**The strongest new additions for a hands-off, very-low-risk buyer near Dublin are businesses that already run without the owner: a Stockton FedEx linehaul with a full-time manager in place ($1.275M asking, ~$408K adjusted cash flow, 3.1x), three East Bay FedEx Ground packages at 3.1–3.9x after a $75K manager, and two UPS Stores (Newark and Napa) at 4.4–4.6x that are pending and worth a backup offer.** Positions 1–30 stay exactly as `top30_calc.py` prints them. Three of them now fail the faith screen and are marked "Excluded – alcohol": #13 (liquor & market), #19 (gas station with beer and wine) and #29 (restaurant with an ABC Type 47 licence). Two others, #28 and #30, need an alcohol check. Positions 31–100 are 70 new listings drawn from about 100 candidates in the notes. Each passes the alcohol, lottery, tobacco, cannabis, gambling, pork and licensed-owner filters, sits within about 1.3 hours of Dublin, and discloses both an asking price and a cash flow. Every row uses the same formulas: cash flow minus a stated manager cost, a 90% SBA loan at the 7(a) maximum variable rate, and an "all-cash yield" (adjusted cash flow ÷ price) for a buyer who wants to avoid interest. Two cautions apply to the whole list. First, cheap headline multiples mostly measure the seller's own unpaid labor. Once a manager is paid, the honest band for low-risk absentee assets is **about 3–7x, or a 15–32% all-cash yield**, not the 1–2x the listings advertise. Second, **every figure comes from search-engine snippets of listing pages that could not be opened, so every row must be verified with the broker before anyone relies on it.**

## One formula, three screens, and a risk-weighted rank

Every row in both tables comes from one script, `research_notes/Top 100 businesses near Dublin/top100_calc.py`. It runs `top30_calc.py` itself and checks, cell by cell, that positions 1–30 match that script's printed table, so the fixed list cannot drift. The formulas are inherited unchanged. **Adjusted cash flow** is listed cash flow minus an estimated manager cost. **Adjusted multiple** is asking price ÷ adjusted cash flow. **Out of pocket** is about 13% of price. The **loan** is 90% of price at the SBA 7(a) variable-rate cap: prime (6.75%) + 6.5% up to $50K, + 6% (12.75%) up to $250K, + 4.5% (11.25%) up to $350K, and + 3% (9.75%) above that ([sba7a.loans](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/what-is-the-sba-7a-loan-interest-rate/)). The loan is repaid over 10 years, and **left after loan** is adjusted cash flow minus the annual payment. The extra **all-cash yield** column is adjusted cash flow ÷ price. It is the return with no loan at all, and so the interest-free view.

Manager costs follow the row type. A FedEx operations manager is $75K, matching the top 30. A hired driver for a single Mission Foods route is $55K, close to the Vallejo-metro driver/sales median of $22.97 an hour plus burden ([Wagedex](https://wagedex.com/jobs/driver-sales-workers/metro/vallejo-ca)). A laundromat attendant is $40K, or $60K for a large attended store. A franchise store or branch manager is $70K–$80K, sitting at California's 2026 exempt-salary floor of about $70,304 ([CalChamber](https://hrwatchdog.calchamber.com/2025/12/california-local-minimum-wage-rates-increases-starting-january-1-2026/)). A small independent shop manager is $55K. A service-company general manager is $80K–$90K. A licensed contractor's qualifying manager (RME), or a licensed PI manager, is $120K–$140K. A preschool or tutoring director is $70K and a manufacturing GM is $100K. Where the listing itself says **"absentee" or "manager in place,"** the script deducts only a **$15K oversight allowance** (bookkeeping, audits, the owner's residual hours), or $30K where the seller still works part-time. Those rows carry an **[A]** marker.

The new 70 were picked in three steps. First, rows with data errors or implausible figures went to the appendix. That covers cash flow above the asking price, figures copied from neighbouring listings, and templated syndications. Second, rows whose cash flow does not cover a manager (adjusted cash flow ≤ 0) were moved below the cut. Third, the rest were sorted by a **score: adjusted multiple × risk weight × absentee factor**. The risk weights are Low 1.0, Low-Med 1.15, Medium 1.3, Med-High 1.5 and High 1.8. The absentee factor is 0.85 when the listing states absentee or manager-run operation. The risk tier includes data confidence, so a FedEx package whose figures conflict across versions is rated Low-Med, not Low. Restaurants whose alcohol status is unknown are rated High. That weighting is why a 3.1x Stockton linehaul with a manager in place ranks above a 2.5x San Jose route package with conflicting numbers, and why no unverified-alcohol restaurant reaches the top 10 of the new block. The pool of about 97 qualifying rows produced 90 that can be ranked. The best 70 fill positions 31–100, and the other 27 are listed in the appendix with full numbers.

## Paying cash turns the list into a 28% riba-free portfolio

SBA 7(a) loans charge interest, so the "Loan" and "Left after loan" columns describe a conventional buyer. For a buyer avoiding riba, the **all-cash yield column is the operative number**. Under the $2M budget, two pairings stand out. The **Stockton manager-run linehaul plus the Napa UPS Store** costs $1.974M and yields about **$560K adjusted, or 28.4%**. The **Contra Costa FedEx package plus the Newark UPS Store** also costs $1.974M and yields about **$556K, or 28.2%**. Both combine one route asset with one franchise retail asset and need only two managers. Both UPS legs are pending, so the second pairing depends on an escrow falling through.

If the buyer does not want to pay all cash, the earlier UPS research identified three interest-free paths. One is a **fixed-price installment sale** from the seller, with a higher price, no interest and no interest-bearing late fees. Another is a **musharaka-style profit share or earn-out**. The third is **murabaha or musharaka financing** from US Islamic providers such as UIF (which absorbed LARIBA in April 2026) or Bank of Whittier ([HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.us/business-financing); [Swoop](https://swoopfunding.com/us/business-loans/islamic-business-finance/)). That research found **no confirmation that these providers fund goodwill-heavy business acquisitions**. Several listings already advertise seller financing, which is the opening for an installment-sale negotiation: the gutter franchise (#43, "$60K down"), the Novato spa (#79, 50% down) and several FedEx packages. A qualified scholar should review the specific contract.

## Conclusion

The ranking shows that, for an owner who will not work in the business, **the market does not offer a very-low-risk asset below about 3x real earnings**. Every listing that looks cheaper is cheap because the seller's own labor is buried in the cash flow (single Mission routes, auto shops, small HVAC books), or because the data is suspect (San Jose FedEx, the pool routes, the restoration company). The useful targets are a small set of manager-run route and franchise assets at 3–4.5x. They throw off 22–32% if bought outright, which makes the interest-free path the stronger one, not a compromise. Two of the best are already pending, and most local UPS and FedEx inventory moves through a handful of brokers (Franchise Clearly, Bay Advisors, RoutesRUs). The practical next step is to get on those brokers' buyer lists with proof of funds and backup-offer standing, not to wait for the public portals.
