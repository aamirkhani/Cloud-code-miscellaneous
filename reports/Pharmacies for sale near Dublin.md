The SF owner is retiring with only 5 employees, and the San Ramon seller will "train and stay," so this report **assumes both owners work as PIC**. That means a buyer replacing them must pay a full salary. The Sacramento Larchmont listing says outright that the "Owner/PIC will stay during transition" ([BusinessesForSale](https://us.businessesforsale.com/us/well-established-retail-pharmacy-in-sacramento.aspx)). **Inventory is the biggest unknown in these numbers.** Both East Bay listings say "FF&E included" but say nothing about drug stock. Pharmacy deals normally pay for inventory **separately, at cost, after outdated and slow stock is pulled** ([Master the Margin](https://masterthemargin.substack.com/p/pharmacy-inventory-and-transactions); [Cencora valuation](https://amerisourcebergen.com/insights/pharmacies/what-goes-into-a-realistic-pharmacy-valuation)). Golden Rose's PDF explicitly adds inventory at cost on top of $750K. The $100K and $250K inventory cases in the output are **illustrative placeholders, not sourced estimates**. They show that **each $100K of inventory adds about 0.2–0.4x to the absentee multiple** and comes straight out of the buyer's cash.

There is a second downside the base case does not capture. If the seller personally covered more than one full-time pharmacist's hours, which is plausible for a 6-day SF store, the buyer also needs a relief pharmacist. A rough extra 0.5 FTE at about $110K loaded is an estimate made for this report, not a sourced figure. On that basis SF falls to about **$323K absentee cash flow (4.7x, 1.5x debt coverage)** and San Ramon to about **$284K (5.2x, 1.36x coverage)**. Both would still be financeable, but no longer cheap.

The small Sacramento stores fail outright. Once a pharmacist is paid, **Larchmont's $205,600 of seller cash flow drops to roughly zero**, while its lease expires about now. The 2017 store reports $100 of cash flow, and the 2022 store's only earnings figure is an unverified $70K. These listings are priced cheaply because their earnings are really the owner's own pharmacist wage. They are not low-multiple bargains for an absentee owner.

## California law allows the buyer to own, but the ownership transfer is the main risk

**California does not require a pharmacy owner to be a pharmacist, but every pharmacy must designate a pharmacist-in-charge** ([terms.law](https://terms.law/CA-Incorporation/pharmacy-corporation.html)). The bar that matters is **BPC §4111**. The Board cannot license (a) prescribers, (b) anyone sharing community property or another financial interest with a prescriber, or (c) a corporation at least 10% owned by such persons ([FindLaw BPC 4111](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-4111/)). The Board applies this to spouses through community-property law and has called its earlier, looser reading a "shallow view of the law" ([CA BOP 2022 materials](https://WWW.PHARMACY.CA.GOV/meetings/agendas/2022/22_jul_lic_mat.pdf)). **If the buyer's spouse, or anyone sharing finances with the buyer, is a physician, dentist or other prescriber, the deal stops here.** The proposed PIC must also have completed the Board's PIC training course within the past two years, a requirement in force since April 1, 2025 ([BOP change-of-PIC form](https://pharmacy.ca.gov/forms/change_of_pic.pdf)).

A **change of ownership (CHOW)** occurs at 50% or more of the beneficial interest. It needs Board approval *before* closing, and the Board then cancels the old license and issues a new number. The buyer either obtains a **temporary permit of up to 180 days** or the pharmacy must stop operating ([BOP application packet](https://www.pharmacy.ca.gov/forms/phy_app_pkt.pdf)). The seller must keep operating and controlling the store until approval, under the Seller's Certification on form 17A-8 ([formsworkflow](https://formsworkflow.com/form/details/161713-california-sellers-certification)). The buyer must also re-establish Medicare (CMS-855S), Medi-Cal, NPI/NCPDP and DEA records for a new entity ([Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/pharmacy-succession-planning-navigating-change-of-ownership-medicare-medicaid-and-pbm-requirements)). Medicare Transaction Facilitator enrollment is now a condition of Part D network participation ([NHIA via Infusion](https://pubs.royle.com/article/Advocacy/4988404/847329/article.html)). **PBM re-credentialing typically takes 60–120 days** with new applications and contracts ([Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/service/change-of-ownership-chow/); [BIPC](https://www.bipc.com/pharmacy-change-of-ownership-transactions-why-chow-must-be-handled-correctly)). That is a stretch in which claims to major payers can stall. Buying the seller's **stock rather than its assets** can preserve the existing payer enrollments and contracts as a change of information. The trade-off is that the buyer **inherits past PBM audit and clawback liability**, which calls for an escrow holdback and indemnities. If the San Ramon store compounds only non-sterile products, as the snippets indicate, it avoids the separate sterile-compounding license and its own CHOW ([BOP sterile packet](https://www.pharmacy.ca.gov/forms/sterile_comp_app_pkt.pdf)). This needs confirming.

**The economics are thin and getting thinner.** Independent gross margin fell to **19.7%, the lowest in NCPA's 10-year lookback** ([Chain Drug Review](https://chaindrugreview.com/ncpa-releases-2024-digest-report/)), down from 21.9% in 2020 ([Drug Channels](https://www.drugchannels.net/2022/02/five-things-to-know-about-state-of.html?m=0)). GLP-1s and other high-cost drugs inflate sales without adding profit ([NCPA 2025](https://ncpa.org/newsroom/news-releases/2025/10/19/ncpa-releases-2025-digest-report)). Since January 1, 2026, Medicare-negotiated **Maximum Fair Prices** have often paid below acquisition cost, and the pharmacy floats the gap until the manufacturer refund arrives through the MTF ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/medicare-prescription-drug-affordability/overview/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program/pharmacy-dispensing-entity-resources)). One vendor's unverified estimate puts that float at **more than $27,000 a month** for an average independent ([IPC](https://www.ipcrx.com/pharmacy-blog/pharmacy-services/navigating-the-2026-cash-flow-crunch-a-survival-guide-for-independent-pharmacy-owners/)). Policy tailwinds exist but should not be paid for. **California SB 41 licenses PBMs and bans spread pricing from 2026** ([Governor's office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/10/11/governor-newsom-signs-sb-41-to-lower-the-cost-of-prescription-drug/)), and federal delinking of Part D PBM pay starts in 2028 ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/congress-passes-significant-federal-pharmacy-benefit-manager-reform-impacting-pharmaceutical-market)).

The upside is **Rite Aid's collapse**. After its second Chapter 11 in May 2025 all stores closed, between about 200 and 347 of them in California depending on the source ([SAN](https://san.com/cc/rite-aid-closes-all-stores-nationwide-after-second-bankruptcy/); [Patch](https://patch.com/california/across-ca/rite-aid-will-close-or-sell-hundreds-stores-ca)). Sacramento independent Pucci's Pharmacy reported higher patient volume afterward ([CBS Sacramento](https://cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/more-sacramento-rite-aids-close-fewer-pharmacy-options-available-for-most-vulnerable)). The buyer should ask each seller how much volume it has already captured and whether a closed Rite Aid sits nearby.

**The halal screen is easier for a pharmacy than for most retail.** **More than 96% of independent pharmacy sales come from the prescription department** ([Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/ncpa-releases-2026-digest-report)), so dropping any beer, wine, tobacco or lottery from the front store costs almost nothing. An off-sale ABC license is separate from the pharmacy license and can simply be left out of an asset purchase. **San Francisco has banned tobacco sales in pharmacies since 2008** ([Drug Topics](https://www.drugtopics.com/view/san-francisco-bans-all-pharmacy-tobacco-sales)), so the SF listing cannot sell it. No snippet mentions alcohol or lottery at either East Bay store; both sit in medical settings, but this is unverified. The LTC and closed-door leads have no front store at all.

## Interest-free financing works, but only with cash on hand

The SBA path in the table charges interest, so it is out for a buyer who wants to avoid riba. **The cleanest riba-free route is an all-cash purchase, and it fits the budget.** SF at $1.53M or San Ramon at $1.48M leaves about **$470K–$520K of a $2M budget** for inventory, CHOW and legal costs, and working capital. The 2026 MFP float alone could absorb a meaningful share of that. The Pacific Business Exchange listing advertises "SBA, cash, or TBD" ([BusinessesForSale](https://us.businessesforsale.com/us/retail-compounding-pharmacy-east-bay-n-california.aspx)), which signals that the seller expects cash at close but may be open to terms.

Three structures avoid interest without paying all cash. The first is a **fixed-price installment sale with the seller** (bay' mu'ajjal). The price may be higher in exchange for deferral, but there is no interest and no interest-bearing late fee. The script's illustration, 40% down and the rest over seven years, needs **about $590K–$610K down and about $127K–$131K a year**, covered about 3.1–3.3 times by absentee cash flow. The second is a **profit-share or earn-out tied to retained scripts**, which also protects the buyer if patients leave after the sale. The third is **musharakah or murabaha financing** from **UIF (which absorbed LARIBA in April 2026)** or **Bank of Whittier's riba-free business financing** ([HalalWallet: Bank of Whittier](https://www.halalwallet.us/business-financing/ca/bank-of-whittier-business-financing); [HalalWallet: UIF](https://www.halalwallet.us/business-financing/ca/university-islamic-financial-corporation-commercial-real-estate-financing)). The limit is that **UIF's confirmed product is commercial real estate ($100K–$2M)**, and no source confirmed that either lender finances **goodwill-heavy pharmacy acquisitions**, which is what a script file is. HalalWallet is also a paid-listing directory. A qualified scholar should approve whichever structure is used.

For comparison, the conventional route is **SBA 7(a) through a pharmacy-specialist lender such as Live Oak Bank**: up to 90% financing, about 45–60 days to fund ([Live Oak](https://www.liveoak.bank/business-loans/sba-loans/?RefId=blog)), and a **10% minimum equity injection under SOP 50 10 8**, where a seller note counts only on full standby ([PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)). **Working capital is the trap for a riba-free owner.** Wholesaler credit terms and revolving lines, the usual way pharmacies carry inventory and the MFP float, are often interest-bearing. Avoiding them means holding a larger cash cushion, and that cash is not earning the 26–29% the store does.

## Conclusion

Seen through an absentee owner's eyes, this market splits cleanly. Pharmacies whose cash flow is mostly the owner's own pharmacist wage collapse once a PIC is hired, and those are exactly the "cheap" ones at 1.3–1.5x. The two Pacific Business Exchange stores have enough volume (about 200 scripts a day) to carry a hired pharmacist and still return about $380K–$445K before financing. That is why they hold up as low-multiple deals at roughly 3.5x after adjustment, even though their headline 2.4–2.6x is the less meaningful number. For this buyer, **the order of operations is: San Ramon first, because it can be supervised from 15 minutes away; SF as the stronger-numbers alternate; the Sacramento LTC closed-door pharmacy as the structurally ideal absentee asset if its price lands near $1M.** Whichever store it is, the deal should only close once three things are in hand: a committed PIC, tax-return-verified earnings, and an inventory figure.

The deeper implication is that this buyer's two preferences pull against each other. Low risk calls for a large cash reserve to cover the CHOW and PBM gap, the MFP float and inventory, while interest-free financing means that reserve cannot come from a wholesaler credit line. An all-cash purchase of one ~$1.5M store plus about $400K–$500K held back as reserves satisfies both, and still yields roughly a quarter of the investment each year on the unverified figures. Paying more than about $1.5M, or stretching to a second store, would be the point where the buyer has to choose between being riba-free and staying low-risk. **All listing figures in this report come from search-result snippets and must be verified with the broker before any offer.**
