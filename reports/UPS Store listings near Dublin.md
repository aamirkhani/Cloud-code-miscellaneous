# Newark tops a thin, mostly pending field

Only two The UPS Store resales within about 1.3 hours of Dublin publish full financials, and both appear to be already under contract. The better one is **Newark (Alameda County, about 25 minutes away)**, asking **$849,000 on $262,363 SDE and $212,363 EBITDA (3.24x SDE, 4.0x EBITDA)** and marked **"Sale Pending"** on BizBuySell. The other is **Napa County**, asking **$699,000 on $222,102 SDE (3.15x)**, which the broker's own site shows as **"Pending"**. This buyer will not work in the store, so a salaried California manager has to replace the owner's labor. That cuts Newark's cash flow to roughly **$172K–$192K (4.4–4.9x)** and Napa's to roughly **$132K–$152K (4.6–5.3x)**. Both still cover a 90% SBA loan about **1.3–1.6 times**, and both return about **18–22% a year if bought all-cash**. The only other branded inventory nearby is a Marin/Sonoma group selling four or five stores at **$470K–$540K each with no published earnings**, a Sacramento store at **$310K with no cash flow disclosed and a lease date already passed**, and several franchisor "store for sale" pages in San Francisco, San Jose and Santa Clara that publish no numbers at all. **Recommendation:** ask broker Franchise Clearly to put the buyer in as a **backup offer on Newark (and on Napa if possible)**. Make any offer conditional on four things: the remaining franchise term (a mandatory remodel costing $97K–$281K would sink the deal), tax-return-verified SDE, a manager who stays, and a riba-free payment structure if the buyer wants one. Use the Marin/Sonoma stores as the fallback. **Every figure here comes from search-engine snippets, because the listing pages themselves were blocked during research. None of it is verified, and two of the best listings are pending.**

**Newark is the standout on paper.** It has the lowest multiple, the most cash flow and the shortest drive. Rent is modest at **$4,131/month (about 8.5% of revenue)**, and the price includes about $75K of FF&E. The store has five employees and is SBA pre-qualified ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/business-opportunity/the-ups-store-franchise-in-alameda-county-ca/2393051/)). The red flag is margin. **SDE of 45% of a $585K revenue** is far above typical. Its two published versions disagree by about $8K on EBITDA.

The two listings share a broker. Their IDs are consecutive. ## A paid manager turns 3.2x into roughly 4.5–5x

The franchisor allows absentee ownership, but each center must be directly supervised by an **on-premises "primary operator" who has completed the new-franchisee and financial-management training** ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/franchises/directory/ups-store-the/282553)). This buyer therefore needs a trained manager. In California, an exempt salaried manager must earn at least **$70,304 a year in 2026** ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2025/12/california-minimum-wage-state-and-local-increases)). With payroll taxes, a loaded cost of **$70K–$90K** is realistic. None of the listings says a manager is already in place, so the table below subtracts that cost from seller SDE. Newark's EBITDA is $50K below its SDE, which suggests a single owner salary was added back, so the deduction appears to fit.

SBA figures assume 10% down, a 10-year term and 9.75%, which gives an annual payment of 15.69% of the loan. Prime + 3%, about 9.75%, is the cap for loans over $350K; loans of $250K–$350K are capped at 11.25% ([sba7a.loans](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/what-is-the-sba-7a-loan-interest-rate/)). SOP 50 10 8 restored the **10% minimum equity injection**, and a seller note counts toward it only on full standby ([PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)). Known franchisor transfer costs are a **$6,000 transfer fee, a $6,000 processing fee and a $7,500 center development fee**, plus a pro-rated renewal fee ([FranchiseEvidence](https://franchiseevidence.com/franchise/the-ups-store/); [Lopes Law](https://lopeslawllc.com/ups-store-fdd-item-6-other-fees/)). That is about **$19.5K before the renewal pro-ration**. Working capital and lender closing costs come on top and are not estimated here.

The Sacramento store ($310K, a $279K loan at the 11.25% cap) and the individual Marin/Sonoma stores ($470K–$540K, about $66K–$76K a year in debt service) cannot be modelled, because they publish no earnings. The Franchise Clearly figure for Napa ($110,500 down) is higher than 10% of the price. It likely includes fees or working capital ([Franchise Clearly](https://members.franchiseclearly.com/seller/recast/summary/25710/public)).

**For a riba-free path,** the SBA loan is out, since it charges interest. Three structures avoid it. The first is a **fixed-price installment sale with the seller**: a higher agreed price paid in equal instalments with no interest and no interest-bearing late fees. The second is a **profit-share (musharaka-style) earn-out**. The third is **murabaha or musharaka financing** from US Islamic providers such as **UIF (which absorbed LARIBA in April 2026)** or **Bank of Whittier's riba-free business financing** ([HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.us/business-financing); [Swoop](https://swoopfunding.com/us/business-loans/islamic-business-finance/)). Research found **no confirmation** that either provider finances goodwill-heavy franchise acquisitions. Bank of Whittier also posts a scam alert, so verify any representative through FDIC/OCC records ([Bank of Whittier](http://www.bankofwhittier.com/lariba-model)).

Here is an illustrative installment sale at the asking price, with 40% down and the balance paid over seven years with no interest. Newark would need **$340K down and about $73K a year**, Napa **$280K down and about $60K a year**, and the pair **$619K down and about $133K a year**. Absentee cash flow covers each about **2.2–2.6 times**. Sellers usually demand a higher price in return for waiving interest. The franchisor must also consent to any seller lien. Both stores are advertised as SBA pre-qualified, which signals the seller expects cash at close. All-cash purchase is the cleanest riba-free route, and at about $0.87M (Newark) or $1.59M (the pair) it fits the budget.

## Franchise rules can add six figures to the price

Ongoing fees take about **8.5% of royalty-bearing sales**: a 5% royalty, a 1% brand marketing fee and a 2.5% national advertising fee capped at $27,734 a year. There is also a **$2,868 annual technology fee** ([FranchiseEvidence](https://franchiseevidence.com/franchise/the-ups-store/)). Some sources say the total is 7.5%, so check Item 6 of the current FDD ([FDDIQ](https://fddiq.com/blog/the-ups-store-franchise-cost-profit)). The franchise term is 10 years, renewable for another 10 at **25% of the then-current initial fee** ([Francloud](https://www.francloud.com/franchises/the-ups-store); [FranchiseChatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2018/02/11/considering-a-ups-store-franchise-dont-overlook-these-25-important-franchise-fees/)). The initial fee itself is reported as either $29,950 or $39,950.

The rule with the biggest effect on value is the transfer remodel. **If the transfer closes within 3.5 years of the seller's franchise expiration, the buyer must complete a full "Laser Lite" remodel within 9 months**. One summary says 11 months ([Scribd FDD 2025](https://www.scribd.com/document/926665318/The-UPS-Store-Inc-Traditional-FDD-2025)). The 2026 FDD estimates the remodel at **$97,047–$281,271** ([FranchiseEvidence](https://franchiseevidence.com/franchise/the-ups-store/)). Take Newark as an example. A mid-range $189K remodel effectively raises its price to about $1.04M against roughly $182K of absentee cash flow, which is **about 5.7x**. Newark was established in 1990 and Napa in 2000, so both are on renewal cycles whose end dates were not disclosed. A buy-side broker says stores needing an upgrade trade at lower multiples than refreshed ones ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/franchise-business-valuation/)). The franchisor also holds a **right of first refusal** on every transfer, must approve the buyer, and requires training before the buyer takes over ([FranchiseEvidence](https://franchiseevidence.com/franchise/the-ups-store/); [UPS Store training](https://www.theupsstorefranchise.com/how-it-works/franchise-training)). Finally, the franchise is tied to a UPS Contract Carrier Agreement, and **the franchise ends automatically if that agreement ends**.

## Flat sales and costly labor are the real risks

The system grows slowly. Item 19 average sales were **$721K in 2023, $720K in 2024 and $724K in 2025**, a real decline after inflation. Only 45% of centers beat the average, and the bottom tenth averaged $346K. Those gross figures also include pass-through UPS shipping charges ([FranchiseEvidence](https://franchiseevidence.com/franchise/the-ups-store/)). A former owner says shipping margins are poor, so print, mailbox and notary services generate the profit ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/The-UPS-Store-Owner-Reviews-EI_IE307820.0,13_KO14,19.htm)). UPS plans to **cut Amazon volume by more than 50% by the second half of 2026** ([Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/ups-amazon-volume-reduction-q4-2024-earnings/738749/)). Meanwhile, free Happy Returns drop-offs add foot traffic and labor without clear per-package pay ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231025012177/en)). The labor cost floor keeps rising: California's minimum wage is $16.90 an hour in 2026, and many localities set it higher ([CalChamber](https://hrwatchdog.calchamber.com/2025/08/californias-2026-minimum-wage-increases-to-16-90-per-hour/)). One 2026 review calls the brand an owner-operator model, not a semi-absentee one ([The Franchise Insiders](https://www.thefranchiseinsiders.com/blog/ups-store-franchise-cost-profit-review-2026)).

These risks are real, but the business itself is well suited to this buyer's values. It sells no alcohol and has no gambling or interest-based products. The one religious-compliance issue is financing.

Lender scrutiny adds another check on Newark's high margin. SBA lenders underwrite to tax returns, so SDE that does not appear in the returns will not support a loan ([Arkansas SBTDC](https://asbtdc.org/buying-a-business-6-things-to-consider/)). A newer SOP, 50 10 8.1, is reported to tighten change-of-ownership rules from October 1, 2026, but only one secondary source reports it ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/23/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-equity-dscr-qoe-guide)).

## Conclusion

The listings make clear that a "low multiple" on a UPS Store is a property of the owner-operator, not of the store. Seller pricing of about 3.2x becomes about 4.5–5x once a California manager is on the payroll, and it rises to nearly 6x if a remodel is triggered. A deal that is still worth doing at that price needs above-median volume, a long remaining franchise term, and ideally two stores sharing one area manager. The Newark-plus-Napa pair, if it really is a single seller and if either deal falls out of escrow, is the only package found that meets all three conditions. It is also the only package that uses most of the $2M budget productively while staying riba-free through an all-cash purchase. If both escrows close, the buyer's real search moves off the public marketplaces. Most local UPS Store inventory sits behind the franchisor's lead forms and Franchise Clearly's private pipeline. The buyer should register with both, and should treat the Marin/Sonoma group as a fallback only after it produces per-store tax returns.
