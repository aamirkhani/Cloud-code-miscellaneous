# Cheap Multiples Hide Expensive Absentee Realities

**Neither listing fits an absentee buyer in Dublin who wants very low risk, and both low multiples disappear once hired labor is paid.** The Vallejo "Mission Foods" listing is a four-route Mission Foods (Gruma) master distributorship with 45 grocery accounts. Its **$451,386 "cash flow" is exactly 18% gross commission on $2,507,700 of sales**, before drivers, trucks, fuel, insurance and stale product. Once four W-2 drivers are paid at Solano County wages, a realistic absentee owner would earn **about $95K to $175K a year (midpoint roughly $135K)**. That is an effective multiple of about 4.5x to 7x on the all-in purchase cost, not 1.37x. Brokers also say financing is generally not available on Mission routes, so the buyer should expect to pay the $619K, plus trucks and four inventory deposits, in cash. The Fairfield full-service car wash ($275K "cash only" on BizQuest, $300K on BizBuySell, ~$141K EBITDA) is a labor-heavy, cash-handling, hand-wash format in a corridor that express tunnels are moving into. After paying a manager, it would likely earn **about $40K to $75K a year**, with California car-wash wage-law exposure on top. The recommendation is to **pass on the car wash** and **not pursue the Vallejo route at its current terms**. The route is worth one disciplined information request, because it is the better of the two businesses. Of the alternatives found, the **$799K semi-absentee Fairfield wash with an assumable $500K EIDL at 3.75%** fits an absentee buyer better on paper. It is still not a low-risk deal. All listing figures come from search-engine extracts of broker pages that could not be opened directly, so every number is unverified.

## What the two listings actually are

### Vallejo: a four-route distributorship priced on gross commission

The Vallejo listing is a Routes R Us listing, "Mission Foods Master Distributorship For Sale - Vallejo CA - $619,000". It is syndicated on BizQuest as BW2368281, with Routes R Us code CA-98750. It covers **four routes and 45 accounts in Vallejo, Napa and American Canyon**, including multiple Safeway, Target, Costco, Walmart, Smart & Final and Raley's locations. Average weekly sales are $48,225, the blended commission is 18%, "annual gross cash flow" is $451,386, and "estimated net income after specified expenses" is $403,026 ([BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-master-distributorship-for-sale-vallejo-ca-619-000/BW2368281)). The arithmetic is exact: $48,225 × 52 = $2,507,700, and 18% of that is $451,386. The "cash flow" is therefore **gross commission, not SDE or EBITDA**. The $403,026 figure implies only about $48K a year of expenses across four routes. The listing states that those expenses exclude route and truck financing. **Two trucks are sold separately, and Mission requires one inventory deposit per route, four in total.** No listing found states the deposit amount ([BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-master-distributorship-for-sale-vallejo-ca-619-000/BW2368281)).

The price is not unusual for this broker. Routes R Us appears to price on a multiple of weekly sales. Its Napa multi-route listing states a "13-1 multiple," and the Vallejo package works out to about 12.8x weekly sales ([BizQuest Napa](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-multi-route-distributorship-for-sale-napa-ca/BW2530493/)). The listing still shows as active and is tagged "Hot Listing." Its BizQuest ID, however, is in the same range as listings from a much older batch, while newer Mission listings carry IDs around 2530000 to 2550000. That suggests a long time on market or a re-posted listing. The Napa accounts may also overlap with Napa routes advertised separately. Both points are inferences that the broker needs to confirm.

### Fairfield: probably the town's oldest hand wash, sold cash only

BizQuest shows a full-service Fairfield car wash asking **$275,000 with about $141,000 EBITDA**. It is described as "the oldest car wash in Fairfield," "priced to sell fast," and a **"cash-only deal"** ([BizQuest Fairfield car washes](https://images.bizquest.com/car-washes-for-sale-in-fairfield-ca)). This is almost certainly the same business seen on BizBuySell at $300,000 and $141,201. The EBITDA matches, and the price gap suggests a price change. The listing ID, broker, revenue, rent, lease term, staff count and reason for sale could not be found. The most plausible identity is **Home Town Car Wash at 1634 N Texas St** ([Checkbook](https://www.checkbook.org/ca/fairfield/car-washes/home-town-car-wash-and-detail/n2ayz)). A 2019 Daily Republic profile described it as a hand-wash (no brushes) business dating to the 1950s that the owner thought "might be the oldest car wash in Solano County," run by an owner who took over in 2015 ([Daily Republic](https://www.dailyrepublic.com/all-dr-news/solano-news/fairfield/gold-mine-april-2019-home-town-car-wash-offers-hands-on-service/)). That identification is unconfirmed until the broker discloses it under NDA.

## Hired labor erases most of both headline numbers

### The route: about $135K, not $451K

Hiring drivers is not optional. Single Mission routes doing $8K to $14K a week take roughly 25 to 40 hours a week. Listings cite about 32 hours in Sacramento, 30 to 40 hours in Grants Pass, and "just 25 hrs/week" in Brooklyn ([BizQuest Sacramento](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-route-for-sale-sacramento-ca-195-000/BW2532569/); [BizQuest Brooklyn](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-route-brooklyn-138k-commissions-just-25-hrs-week/BW2532067/)). The Vallejo package averages about $12K a week per route, so an absentee owner needs about four drivers. In the Vallejo metro, the BLS median for driver/sales workers is **$22.97 an hour** ([Wagedex](https://wagedex.com/jobs/driver-sales-workers/metro/vallejo-ca)), and a local delivery-driver posting pays $19 to $21 ([Express Pros](https://jobs.expresspros.com/job/14578871)).

At $22.97 plus an assumed 15% for payroll tax and workers' comp, four drivers cost about **$165K a year at 30 hours each, or $220K at full time**. For the operating base, single-route listings that report a "net" run about 13% to 14% of sales, after fuel, insurance, vehicle and stale costs. Examples include Encinitas ($87,947 on $640,817) and Clovis ($88,244 on $621,868) ([BizQuest Encinitas](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/missions-tortilla-route-encinitas-ca/BW2512067/); [BizBuySell Fresno-area](https://www.bizbuysell.com/california/five-points/wholesale-and-distribution-businesses-for-sale/)). Applying 13.5% to Vallejo's sales gives about **$339K before labor**.

The table below brackets the absentee result. It adds an assumed $15K to $25K for a lead-driver premium, payroll service, bookkeeping and employer liability coverage.

| Absentee earnings build (Vallejo) | Conservative | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | $339K (13.5% cross-check) | $403K (broker "net") |
| Four W-2 drivers, burdened | −$220K (full time) | −$165K (30 hrs) |
| Lead premium, payroll, bookkeeping, insurance (assumed) | −$25K | −$15K |
| **Absentee owner earnings, pre-debt** | **~$95K** | **~$223K** |

The optimistic column assumes the broker's $48K of "specified expenses" really covers trucks, fuel, insurance and stales for four routes, which is unlikely. **A defensible planning figure is $120K to $150K.** Stale product cuts into that further. Mission does not credit stale or out-of-date product, and the distributor is liable for inventory discrepancies ([RoutesRUs Napa](https://routesrus.com/mission-foods-napa-ca-ca-41877/)). Code-date rotation depends on how diligent each hired driver is, and that is exactly what an owner living in Dublin cannot watch.

### The car wash: about $40K to $75K once a manager is paid

Full-service washes are the most labor-intensive format. Operators put fully burdened labor at about 40% to 50% of sales, and one says absentee owners usually run near 50% ([CarWashForum](https://carwashforum.com/threads/ideal-labor-percent-of-sales.12245); [CarWashForum](https://www.carwashforum.com/threads/avg-profit-margin-from-each-car-wash.14113)). Bay Area car-wash site manager postings run **$49,920 to $83,200** plus commission ([EnergyHire Fremont](https://dev.energyhire.com/job-board/car-wash-site-manager-3008-fremont-ca-6b84e8ff-057a-4f8c-82fb-890f3974be66); [EnergyHire San Jose](https://dev.energyhire.com/job-board/car-wash-site-manager-3003-san-jose-ca-c39dc16d-dca5-4a31-96e4-daec0fa39589)). A salaried manager must earn at least about $70,304 to be exempt from overtime, because California's 2026 minimum wage is $16.90 and the exempt salary floor is twice that for full-time work ([CalChamber](https://hrwatchdog.calchamber.com/2025/12/california-local-minimum-wage-rates-increases-starting-january-1-2026/)).

If the $141K EBITDA reflects a hands-on owner who is not drawing a salary, which is likely for a decade-long owner-operated hand wash, a burdened manager at about $70K to $90K cuts it to **about $50K to $70K**. A capex reserve for pumps, dryers and vacuums lowers it further. So do Fairfield water rates, which are scheduled to rise about 8% a year through 2030 ([Daily Republic](https://www.dailyrepublic.com/news/fairfield-reminds-residents-water-rates-go-up-in-2026/article_b0e6ea63-94d5-4ce0-9ec3-84a33ca58765.html)). The market price points the same way. Small car washes sold at **4.12x SDE in 2024 and 4.73x in 2025** ([BizBuySell benchmark](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/car-washes/)). At those multiples, a $275K to $300K price implies the market believes in roughly **$60K to $73K of sustainable earnings**, not $141K.

## Cash, financing and what survives debt service

Earlier analysis assumed SBA 7(a) terms of 10% down and a 10-year loan at about 9.75% to 11.25%. Under the current rules, variable rates are capped at Prime + 3%, about 9.75%, for loans above $350K, and at 11.25% for loans of $250,001 to $350,000 ([sba7a.loans](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/what-is-the-sba-7a-loan-interest-rate/)). SOP 50 10 8 set a 10% minimum equity injection, and goodwill is financed over no more than 10 years ([Grow America](https://growamerica.org/2026/06/16/what-is-an-equity-injection-and-why-it-can-make-or-break-an-sba-7a-loan/); [CVBBA](https://www.cvbba.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CVBBA-SBA-Fact-Sheet-v3-FINAL-JULY-2025.pdf)). **The new research undercuts that assumption for both deals.** Routes R Us states that "financing is NOT available on Mission Food routes," its Napa sister listing says "Company financing available: No," and no SBA lender was found that finances Mission routes ([RoutesRUs Sacramento](https://routesrus.com/mission-foods-sacramento-ca-ca-70543/); [BizQuest Napa](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-multi-route-distributorship-for-sale-napa-ca/BW2530493/)). The Fairfield wash is advertised as **"cash only"** ([BizQuest](https://images.bizquest.com/car-washes-for-sale-in-fairfield-ca)).

The SBA rows in the table below are therefore hypothetical. They show what would remain if a lender did step in. All loan payments are calculated at 10-year full amortization.

| | Vallejo route | Fairfield wash ($275K–$300K) | Fairfield $799K wash (comp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Realistic financing | Cash; HELOC or seller note at best | Cash only | SBA (30% down) or assume $500K EIDL at 3.75% |
| Cash at close | $619K + 2 trucks + 4 deposits + working capital (likely $700K+) | $275K–$300K + working capital + DD/legal | ~$250K–$300K down if EIDL assumed |
| Absentee earnings, pre-debt | ~$95K–$175K (plan on ~$135K) | ~$40K–$75K | ~$135K–$155K after a manager, before the rent step-up |
| Hypothetical SBA: 90% loan, 10 yr | $557K at 9.75% = **~$87K/yr** | $248K–$270K at 11.25% = **~$41K–$45K/yr** | n/a |
| Left after hypothetical SBA debt | ~$8K–$88K (DSCR ~1.1–2.0x) | ~$0–$34K (DSCR ~1.0–1.8x) | n/a |
| All-cash yield on cash invested | ~14%–25% | ~13%–27% | n/a |

On the Vallejo route, an all-cash buyer earning about $135K on roughly $700K is getting about a 19% pre-tax yield. That is reasonable for a distribution business, but it is not a 1.37x bargain, and the capital is tied to one supplier's contract. On the car wash, an all-cash buyer is risking about $300K on an earnings figure the market prices at half of what the listing claims. Even under the hypothetical SBA terms, the wash's coverage would sit near the 1.10x minimum that applies to small 7(a) loans ([MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/sba-car-wash-feasibility-study)).

## The real risks are concentrated in contracts, labor law and competition

### Route risk: the supplier holds the switch

A Mission distributor signs a "Store Door Distributor Agreement" governed by Texas law with an arbitration clause. Gruma has terminated distributors before: in *Adler v. Gruma*, the Third Circuit vacated an order compelling arbitration in April 2025 ([Public Justice](https://www.publicjustice.net/case/adler-v-gruma/); [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/adler-v.-gruma:-faa-1-exemption-for-interstate-distributors-&-new-jersey-choice-of-law-principles/view)). Mission must approve every transfer. It requires an LLC or corporation and an approved distributor agreement before closing, and it may run credit and background checks ([RoutesRUs Sacramento](https://routesrus.com/mission-foods-sacramento-ca-ca-37521/)). Gruma negotiates chain-store prices that distributors must honor. In 2020 it paid **$5M to settle claims from about 900 California distributors** over misclassification and resale price maintenance ([BakingBusiness](https://www.bakingbusiness.com/articles/51345-gruma-to-pay-5-million-in-settlement-with-distributors)). In 2018 Gruma itself warned that changes to the employee-contractor test could force changes to its distribution system ([Gruma](https://www.gruma.com/media/685398/press_release_-_independent_contractors.pdf)).

For an absentee owner, three more risks stack on top. As an employer of drivers, the owner carries California wage-and-hour duties: overtime, meal and rest breaks, and expense reimbursement. Account concentration in a handful of chains means a single store reset or lost account hits revenue directly. And because lenders do not finance these routes, the eventual buyer pool is limited to cash buyers. No source confirmed whether Mission's agreement permits a fully absentee distributor with hired drivers. That single question decides whether the deal works at all.

### Car wash risk: format, wage law and the tunnel down the street

California requires car-wash employers to register annually and post a **$150,000 surety bond** ([AB 1387](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/13-14/bill/asm/ab_1351-1400/ab_1387_bill_20131011_chaptered.htm)). The Labor Commissioner enforces this hard. In December 2024 it issued about $1.3M in penalties against 19 washes ([DIR](https://dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2024/2024-102.html)), and the 2019 Playa Vista citation of about $2.36M named the owners personally ([DIR](https://dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2019/2019-37.html)). Cash-heavy full-service sites also have well-documented manager skimming cases, including one manager who deleted POS sales and took more than $100K ([PC&D](https://www.carwash.com/skimmer-problem-plagues-carwash-owner/)).

Competition is the structural problem. Quick Quack opened an express tunnel on Holiday Lane in 2021 ([Fairfield-Suisun Chamber](https://business.fairfieldsuisunchamber.com/events/details/ribbon-cutting-quick-quack-car-wash-7506)). 7 Flags runs both an express site at 1206 N Texas St and a full-service site at 2270 N Texas St, on the same corridor as the likely target ([Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/7-flags-express-carwash-fairfield-fairfield); [7 Flags](https://7flagscarwash.com/locations/fairfield)). Industry growth is flowing to express tunnels and subscription memberships ([Mordor Intelligence](https://mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/car-wash-market); [PC&D](https://www.carwash.com/ica-q1-pulse-report/)). An old hand wash also leaves open questions about the remaining lease term, the condition of its clarifier and sewer discharge setup, and possible environmental contamination. Fairfield's sewer district regulates car-wash discharge under its pretreatment ordinance ([FSSD](https://www.fairfieldsuisunsewer.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Ord-1701-1.pdf)).

## Alternatives that fit an absentee buyer better, and what to ask

The other Fairfield wash (listing 2089342) is the most interesting alternative. It is a remodeled, "semi-absentee" full-service wash asking **$799K on $225K cash flow and $450K revenue**, with 6 employees, a lease running to October 2039, SBA eligibility at 30% down, and an **assumable $500K SBA EIDL at 3.75% fixed** ([LoopNet](https://www.loopnet.com/biz/Business-Opportunity/full-service-car-wash-for-sale-in-fairfield-california/2089342/)). BizBen shows it at $250K down ([BizBen](https://www.bizben.com/business-for-sale/full-service-car-wash-for-sale-in-fairfield-california-280024.php)). If the EIDL carries about 30 years of amortization (an assumption to verify), it would cost only about $28K a year.

The listing's own text, however, says rent of about $5,400 a month plus NNN rises to **$7,950 a month after two years with 3% annual increases**. That step-up alone removes about $30K a year. Other versions of the listing show conflicting rent and acreage figures, and a 50% cash-flow margin is high for a full-service wash. On paper, after a manager and the rent step-up, it could clear roughly $80K to $100K over the EIDL payment on about $250K to $300K down. That is a better absentee profile than the $275K wash, but it inherits the same wage-law and tunnel-competition risks, and the SBA must consent to the EIDL assumption.

Single Mission routes are not a safer substitute for an absentee owner. The Napa single route asks $90K on $83,697 and Vacaville asks $105K on $97,852, now pending sale ([BizQuest Napa](https://images.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-route-for-sale-napa-ca-90-000/BW2435645); [BizQuest Vacaville](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-route-for-sale-vacaville-ca-105-000-sale-pending/BW2476487)). One burdened driver at about $40K to $55K consumes most of a single route's net, leaving perhaps $15K to $35K. The Napa multi-route listing ($300K, $210,246 gross, 8 accounts) faces the same two-driver arithmetic. These routes suit owner-operators. Earlier research also flagged FedEx Ground route packages, such as Hayward's 8 routes at 3.15x EBITDA, as businesses built around hired drivers and managers ([BizBuySell Bay Area routes](https://www.bizbuysell.com/california/san-francisco-bay-area/routes-for-sale/)). They deserve a separate look if the buyer wants route-style income with lender financing.

Before spending money on diligence for either listing, the buyer should get written answers to the questions below.

| Topic | Vallejo Mission route | Fairfield car wash ($275K/$300K) |
|---|---|---|
| Status and identity | Is CA-98750 still active, how long has it been listed, has the price changed, and are the Napa accounts still included? | Business name and address, listing ID, why it's $275K vs $300K, reason for sale |
| Earnings proof | 24 months of Mission commission statements, 3 years of tax returns, the line items behind $403,026 | 3 years of tax returns and POS/car-count data; is $141K before or after owner salary? |
| Labor | How many routes the owner drives; current drivers' pay, W-2 vs 1099, hours; will they stay? | Headcount, pay method (no flat day rates), timecards, tip records, any DLSE claims |
| Contract and compliance | Copy of the Store Door Distributor Agreement: termination and cure terms, transfer fee, whether hired drivers and an absentee owner are allowed; written Mission approval as a closing condition | Car-wash registration and $150K bond; FSSD discharge permit and violation history |
| Assets and extras | Truck prices and condition; per-route deposit amount; is a truck needed for each route? | Equipment ages and an independent inspection; reclaim system; capex history |
| Premises | n/a | Lease term, renewal options, rent, NNN charges, environmental indemnity, Phase I |
| Deal terms | Any seller note; stale-product history by account; a non-compete; a training period | Will the seller carry a note? Escrow holdback for wage claims; CDTFA/EDD clearances |

## Conclusion

The headline multiples in both listings measure different things than a buyer would assume. The Vallejo figure is a commission rate times sales, and the Fairfield figure is most likely an owner-operator's earnings. Restated for an owner who lives in Dublin and hires all the labor, the "1.37x route" becomes roughly a 5x deal that must be paid in cash and depends on Gruma's consent and goodwill. The "2x car wash" becomes roughly a 4x to 6x deal with exposure to personal wage-law liability. For a buyer whose priorities are very low risk and a low real multiple, the right answer for **#2 is a clear pass**. For **#1 the answer is to pass at the current terms**, but send one focused request: the distributor agreement, the commission statements and the owner's driver-labor details. Reopen the route only if Mission confirms in writing that absentee ownership is allowed, a driver-paid P&L shows at least about $150K, and the all-in price (including trucks and deposits) comes down toward roughly 4x those verified earnings.

The broader lesson for this search is that, among businesses under $2M, a genuinely absentee-friendly deal is usually one that already pays a manager and drivers in its reported numbers. The low-multiple listings rarely do. A buyer filtering for absentee fit should screen for businesses whose stated cash flow already reflects paid management and staff, as the $799K Fairfield wash claims to, then verify that claim, rather than filtering on the headline multiple.
