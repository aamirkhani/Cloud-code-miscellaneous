# Manager-Run Routes Beat Cheap Owner Jobs

**Only 83 listings within about 1.3 hours of Dublin pass every screen and still earn at least $40K a year after a hired manager is paid, so this is a ranked list of 83, not 100.** Ranked from scratch with one formula, the strongest fits for a hands-off, very-low-risk Muslim buyer are the FedEx route packages that already have managers. The best is a **Stockton FedEx linehaul with a full-time manager in place: $1.275M asking, about $408K adjusted cash flow, 3.1x, a 32% all-cash yield and 2.3x debt coverage** ([BusinessBroker.net Stockton](https://www.businessbroker.net/city/stockton-ca-businesses-for-sale.aspx)). Close behind are **Bay Advisors' Solano package with a manager and a 12-person team (3.5x)** ([Bay Advisors](https://thebayadvisors.com/profitable-fedex-routes-for-sale-san-francisco-bay-area/)) and its **SBA-pre-qualified Contra Costa package (3.1x after a $75K manager)** ([Bay Advisors CC](https://thebayadvisors.com/fedex-routes-sba-pre-qualified-newer-fleet-sales-of-2-1m-contra-costa-country/)). Three small listings score even better on paper: a manager-run South Bay auto-service franchise (1.6x), an Express Employment franchise in South San Francisco (1.7x) and a manager-operated SF corporate caterer (2.3x). All three carry more operating risk, and the caterer still needs an alcohol check. The corrections from later research reshuffle the old list. The **Vallejo Mission Foods route drops from #1 to #43** once its "cash flow" is treated as gross commission. The **Fairfield car wash falls to #32**. The **S&S Welding and pizza listings leave the ranking** because their cash flow exceeds the price or their menus are pork-heavy. The **San Mateo boba franchise also drops out**, because a manager-paid P&L cannot reach $40K at any plausible sales level. For an interest-free buyer, two businesses bought outright, such as the **Stockton linehaul plus a $350K Solano laundromat ($1.625M, about $514K a year, 31.6%)**, deliver more than any single listing. **Every figure comes from search-result snippets of listing pages that could not be opened, so each one must be verified with the broker.**

## One formula ranks every listing, with no fixed positions

The pool is every listing in the six research folders and six earlier reports that sits within about 80 minutes' off-peak drive of Dublin, asks $2M or less, and shows both an asking price and a cash-flow figure. Rows that appear in more than one file are counted once. Earlier positions carry no weight: the old top 30 was re-scored alongside everything else. Every row, its source and every column are computed by `research_notes/Top 100 near Dublin reranked/rerank_calc.py`, and the tables below are its unedited output.

**Adjusted cash flow** is the stated cash flow minus the cost of replacing the owner. The deduction depends on the type of business:

| Business type | Deduction |
|---|---|
| FedEx route packages | $75K operations manager, or $15K oversight where the listing says a manager is in place |
| Franchise store or branch | $75K manager, near California's 2026 exempt-salary floor of $70,304 ([CalChamber](https://hrwatchdog.calchamber.com/2025/12/california-local-minimum-wage-rates-increases-starting-january-1-2026/)) |
| Laundromat | $40K attendant; $15K oversight if absentee-run; $30K where the seller still works part-time |
| Licensed contractor | $120K–$150K licensed RME |
| Car wash | $60K site manager, in line with Bay Area postings of $49.9K–$83.2K ([EnergyHire Fremont](https://dev.energyhire.com/job-board/car-wash-site-manager-3008-fremont-ca-6b84e8ff-057a-4f8c-82fb-890f3974be66)) |
| Pharmacy where the owner is pharmacist-in-charge | $200K for a hired PIC, against a $177,410 metro pharmacist median ([Wagedex](https://wagedex.com/jobs/pharmacists/metro/san-francisco-oakland-fremont-ca)); inventory is extra |
| Listing states absentee or manager in place | $15K oversight |

Mission Foods routes get a heavier correction because their "cash flow" is the distributor's gross commission. In Vallejo it is exactly 18% of $2,507,700 in sales ([BizQuest BW2368281](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/mission-foods-master-distributorship-for-sale-vallejo-ca-619-000/BW2368281)). Each route therefore loses a $55K driver, close to the Vallejo driver/sales median of $22.97 an hour plus burden ([Wagedex](https://wagedex.com/jobs/driver-sales-workers/metro/vallejo-ca)), and 5% of sales for truck, fuel, insurance and stale product. Vallejo uses the dedicated report's realistic planning figure of about **$135K** (range $95K–$175K); the bare formula would give about $106K. Where a single route did not disclose its sales, the script estimated them as cash flow ÷ 18%, the commission rate seen on the Vallejo and Napa listings. That estimate is why every single Mission route falls below $40K.

Three boba listings were tested against the earlier P&L model, which pays a manager and uses San Mateo's $18.60 or the $20 fast-food wage ([DIR FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Fast-Food-Minimum-Wage-FAQ.htm)). Cupertino's claimed $256K on $592K of sales (a 43% margin) is cut to about **$98K**. That figure is the modelled $155K–$191K before rent, less an assumed ~$60K rent and $15K oversight. San Mateo's claimed $180K is cut to about **$24K**, the model's result at $600K of sales, which sends it to the appendix.

Each row also shows four financing measures. An **SBA 7(a) loan** covers 90% of the price over 10 years at the maximum variable rate: 12.75% up to $250K, 11.25% from $250K to $350K, and 9.75% above that ([sba7a.loans](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/what-is-the-sba-7a-loan-interest-rate/)). **Out-of-pocket** is 13% of the price, plus inventory or trucks where the listing names them. **Debt-service coverage (DSCR)** is adjusted cash flow divided by the annual payment. **Financeable** reads "SBA-likely" at a DSCR of 1.25 or more and "SBA-marginal" below it. It overrides both to "cash-only" or "no financing" where the listing says so, as the Mission routes and the Fairfield wash do.

**The score** is the adjusted multiple times four factors, and lower is better:

| Factor | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Risk tier | Low 1.0, Low-Med 1.15, Medium 1.3, Med-High 1.6, High 2.0 |
| Listing states absentee, semi-absentee or manager in place ([A]) | × 0.85 |
| Status is stale, or figures conflict across sources ([S]) | × 1.15 |

A price-to-earnings ratio alone would reward thin, fragile businesses. The risk weight makes an owner-labor-heavy or wage-law-exposed business pay for its fragility: a 2.8x salon has to beat a 3.5x FedEx package by enough to cover its 30% risk penalty. The [A] factor rewards listings that already work without the owner. The [S] factor charges for data the buyer cannot yet trust.

The tiers reflect operating risk for an absent owner. FedEx route packages with a contract and drivers are Low. UPS-type franchises and laundromats are Low-Med. Service companies, pharmacies (with their change-of-ownership and payer risk) and contractors are Medium. Auto shops, car washes (wage-law exposure), vending, staffing start-ups and businesses with very short histories are Med-High. Restaurants are High.

A row needs at least **$40K of adjusted cash flow** to rank. Of the 83 that do, 21 are Low or Low-Med risk, and 15 carry a faith "verify" flag: mostly restaurants and caterers whose alcohol status is unknown, plus boba toppings, two food or wholesale product lines and the pharmacies' front stores.

## The top ten: three cheap small deals, then FedEx packages with managers

**#1, the South Bay auto-service franchise ($145K asking, $105K stated, about $90K adjusted, 1.6x)**, ranks first because its listing says a manager and team are already in place ([DealStream auto](https://dealstream.com/california/santa-clara-county/auto-repair-businesses-for-sale)). It is still a technician-dependent shop rated Med-High, it comes from a single snippet with no brand, and a 72% return on price is the kind of figure that often shrinks once the books are opened. Buy it only on tax returns. **#2, Express Employment in South San Francisco ($425K, $319K stated, 1.7x after a $75K branch manager)**, has the strongest debt coverage of any row, at 4.1x ([BizBuySell San Mateo](https://www.bizbuysell.com/california/san-mateo-county-businesses-for-sale/)). Staffing, however, means funding weekly payroll before clients pay, carrying workers' comp, and depending on a few employer accounts. **#3, the manager-operated SF corporate caterer ($325K, 2.3x)**, ranks high on paper but must first show that it never serves alcohol at events ([BizBuySell SF Co.](https://www.bizbuysell.com/california/san-francisco-county-businesses-for-sale/)).

From #4 onward the list matches the buyer's brief. The **Stockton linehaul (#4)** and the **Solano 13-vehicle package with a "dedicated BC manager and a team of 12" (#5, $1.4M, about $397K adjusted, 3.5x)** are the only Low-risk assets whose listings say management is already in place. Next comes the **Contra Costa Bay Advisors package (#6, $1.125M, 3.1x, 32% all-cash)**, although that broker marks many of its listings sold, so its status needs checking first. The **San Jose 13-route package (#7)** screens cheapest of all the FedEx rows at 2.5x and a 40% yield. Its figures conflict across BizBen versions and two older postings are inactive, so the discount may hide a fleet or contract problem ([BizBen 288357](https://www.bizben.com/business-for-sale/fedex-ground-routes-for-sale-in-san-jose-california-288357)). The **San Leandro 10-route group (#8, $1.0M, 3.5x)** shares a listing with a 13-route option at #14 ([BizBen 289956](https://www.bizben.com/business-for-sale/fedex-ground-routes-for-sale-in-san-leandro-california-289956)).

**#9, the $220K owner-absentee laundromat in Santa Clara County (2.8x)**, is the cheapest absentee laundry. Its rent is about 41% of revenue, it is sold cash-only with "no financials", and its staffing claims conflict ([BizBen 2134986](https://www.bizben.com/business-for-sale/owner-absentee-laundromatbusy-area-with-parking-2134986)). **#10, the Marin hair salon and beauty school (2.8x after a $120K manager)**, has strong numbers. It depends on stylists and enrollment, though, and the snippet names no manager.

A buyer who wants very low risk above all should read the list through the risk column rather than the rank. Twenty-one rows are Low or Low-Med. Twelve of them are FedEx packages at **2.5x–5.5x adjusted** (#4–8, #14, #17, #21, #22, #27, #34 and #42). The rest are the **Solano $350K laundromat (#12, 3.3x, cash-only sale, lease to 2031)** ([BizBen Solano](https://www.bizben.com/business-for-sale/solano-county-california-ca)), the Napa sign franchise, the Marin/Sonoma UPS bundle and six laundromats at 6.6x–9.6x.

## FedEx packages offer the best mix of low risk and low price

The category view below explains the ranking. **FedEx routes** are the deepest pool, with 12 ranked packages at a median of 4.0x and a 25% all-cash yield, and every one is SBA-likely. Their risks sit outside the business itself: FedEx contract renewals, fleet age, and the fact that several of the best are sold or of uncertain status.

**Pharmacies** look cheap at about 3.5–3.7x even after a $200K pharmacist-in-charge ([BusinessesForSale SF](https://us.businessesforsale.com/us/retail-pharmacy-in-san-francisco.aspx); [BusinessesForSale San Ramon](https://us.businessesforsale.com/us/retail-compounding-pharmacy-east-bay-n-california.aspx)). Three things weigh on them. Inventory is paid on top of the price. Change-of-ownership and payer re-credentialing take months. Both listings also carry conflicting figures, so they land at #28 (SF) and #36 (San Ramon).

**Laundromats** do what an absentee owner wants, but sellers charge for it. The eight ranked laundries have a median of **6.9x and 14%**, and most leave almost nothing after an SBA payment. They suit an all-cash buyer and barely work with a loan. The two exceptions are the Solano store (#12) and the Santa Clara County store (#9).

**Franchises** are mixed. The auto-service, Express Employment and gutter franchises (#1, #2, #15) earn well. The UPS bundle (#50), the Modesto juice bar (#51) and ServiceMaster (#68) are thin once a manager is paid. Several smaller franchise resales fall below $40K altogether: the juice bar, float spa, bakery, junk hauling, Marin home care and both mail-center franchises.

**Car washes** rank #32 for the $275K Fairfield hand wash, which is cash-only and which the dedicated report sized at only $40K–$75K net, and #69 for the $799K semi-absentee wash. A buyer who does not want to pay all cash has three interest-free routes, all identified in the earlier UPS research. The first is a **fixed-price installment sale** from the seller, with no interest and no interest-bearing late fees. The second is a **musharaka-style profit share or earn-out**. The third is **murabaha or musharaka financing from UIF (which absorbed LARIBA in April 2026) or Bank of Whittier** ([HalalWallet](https://www.halalwallet.us/business-financing); [Swoop](https://swoopfunding.com/us/business-loans/islamic-business-finance/)). That research found **no confirmation that these providers fund goodwill-heavy business purchases**. Listings that already offer seller terms, such as the gutter franchise ("$60K down"), the Novato spa (50% seller financing) and several FedEx packages with vehicle notes, are the natural places to propose an installment sale. A qualified scholar should review the specific contract.

## Conclusion

Re-ranking from scratch changes which kind of deal comes out on top. The old list was led by headline multiples that turned out to measure seller labor (single Mission routes) or gross commission (Vallejo). Once both are corrected, the cheap end of the market holds two kinds of business. One is a small owner-run operation that pays a modest wage, which an absentee buyer cannot use. The other is a manager-run route or franchise at roughly **3–4x real earnings (25–32% all-cash)**. No listing found offers a very-low-risk business much below 3x. Every sub-2.5x row either rests on a single unverified snippet (#1, #7) or carries real operating exposure (#2, #3, #9).

Even the best FedEx targets need broker confirmation first: several are marked sold or have conflicting figures, and the two UPS Stores are already pending. The highest-value next step is therefore to move ahead of public inventory. That means joining the buyer lists of Bay Advisors, BusinessBroker.net's Stockton listing agent and Franchise Clearly with proof of funds, offering all cash or an interest-free installment structure, and asking for backup-offer standing on Newark and Napa.
