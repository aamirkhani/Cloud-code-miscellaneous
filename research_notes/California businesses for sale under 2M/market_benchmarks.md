# California Small-Business-for-Sale Market Benchmarks (≤ $2M), as of Oct 2026

Method note: research done 2026-10-09 by web search only. Direct page fetches of bizbuysell.com, smallbiztrends.com and beancount.io were blocked by the network proxy, so the figures below come from search-result extracts of those pages and from secondary summaries. Primary BizBuySell numbers should be spot-checked on bizbuysell.com/insight-report before publication. BizBuySell "cash flow" = Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE). BizBuySell multiples are **averages**, while its dollar figures are **medians**. Do not mix the two.

## 1. BizBuySell Insight Report: US and California medians and multiples (2025 to 2026)

### Takeaway
The national closed-deal benchmark has been very stable. In Q2 2026, 2,117 businesses sold at a median sale price of about $349K, median revenue of about $692K and median SDE of about $156K. The average multiple was 2.7x SDE and 0.7x revenue, and deals closed at about 94% of asking (2025 figure). I found no statewide California closed-sale median for 2025 or 2026. California asking-price medians are about $360K.

### Cited Findings
**US, Q2 2026 (Apr to Jun 2026, latest quarter found)**
- 2,117 businesses changed hands, down 10% both QoQ and YoY. Total enterprise value was about $1.8B. — [BizBuySell Insight Report](https://bizbuysell.com/insight-report); [Small Business Trends](https://smallbiztrends.com/small-business-acquisitions-drop-10-as-buyers-get-more-selective/)
- Median sale price was $349,250 (down 1% YoY). Median cash flow was $155,921 (down 3%). Median revenue was $692,087 (down 3%). — [Small Business Trends](https://smallbiztrends.com/small-business-acquisitions-drop-10-as-buyers-get-more-selective/); [StartupOwl](https://startupowl.com/fund/business-valuation-calculator)
- The average cash-flow multiple was 2.7x (up 2% YoY). The average revenue multiple was about 0.7x (flat). — [Small Business Trends](https://smallbiztrends.com/small-business-acquisitions-drop-10-as-buyers-get-more-selective/)
- Q2 2026 sector figures:
  - Service was 40% of deals. Service volume fell 11%, the median sale price held at $350,000, and median days on market (DOM) improved 9% to 155 days.

The average cash-flow multiple was 2.61x (up 1%), and the revenue multiple was 0.69x (up 2%). — [BizBuySell 2025 Year in Review](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/)
- Florida, California, Texas, Arizona and New York had the highest buyer demand in 2025. This measures buyer interest, not prices. — [Business Exits summary](https://businessexits.com/bizbuysells-2025-market-recap-what-the-data-shows-for-business-sellers/)

**California**
- BizBuySell's California market overview shows about 5,500 businesses for sale, with a median asking price of $360,000. The date is undated but appears to be from 2026. — [BizBuySell California listings](https://www.bizbuysell.com/california-businesses-for-sale/4/)
- In 2025, the Greater Sacramento area had 83 reported business sales, with a median sale price of $250,000. This is metro-level data only. — [Sacramento Business Brokers](https://www.sacramentobusinessbrokers.com/post/selling-a-business-in-2026-what-the-latest-market-data-tells-owners)

### Inferences
- A "typical" US closed deal in 2025 and 2026 runs about $350K in price, about $700K in revenue and about $155K in SDE, at about 2.6 to 2.7x SDE and about 0.7x revenue. This has been flat for six-plus quarters, so volume, not price, is what is adjusting.
- The ~$360K CA median asking price is close to the national median *sale* price. At ~94% sale/ask, CA closed deals would land around $340K. This is an estimate, not a reported figure.
- Q2 2026's 2,117 deals, down 10% QoQ, are consistent with the Q1 2026 figure of about 2,345.
- DOM benchmarks: about 155 to 200 days for most small deals, longer for manufacturing (207 to 247). A buyer should expect 5 to 8 months from listing to close.

### Gaps
- No statewide California closed-sale median, median SDE or multiple for 2025 or 2026 was found. BizBuySell publishes state and metro data inside the full Insight Report, but those pages could not be fetched.
- No Q2 2026 national DOM or sale-to-asking ratio was found, and no Q3 2026 report was found (it is likely due mid/late October 2026).

## 2. SDE and revenue multiples by industry and by price band (deals under $2M)

### Takeaway
Most sub-$2M deals price at about 2.0 to 3.3x SDE. Multiples rise with size: about 2.0 to 2.3x under $500K, about 2.5 to 2.8x for $500K to $1M, and about 3.0 to 3.3x for $1M to $2M. Restaurants sit lowest (about 2.1 to 2.3x SDE, about 0.4x revenue). Retail is about 2.6 to 2.7x, and manufacturing is highest among Main Street sectors at about 3.0x.

### Cited Findings
**By industry (BizBuySell closed-deal data)**
- Manufacturing: 5-year (2021 to 2025) average of about 3.00x SDE and about 0.72x revenue. 2025 was 3.02x and 0.70x. — [BizBuySell manufacturing benchmarks](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/manufacturing/)
- Retail: 5-year average of about 2.62x SDE and about 0.53x revenue, rising from 2.49x (2021) to 2.74x (2025). The interquartile range is 1.62 to 3.08x. — [BizBuySell retail benchmarks](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/retail-business/)
- Food/restaurants (2025 snapshot): about 2.33x cash flow and about 0.41x revenue, against manufacturing at 3.07x and retail at 2.72x in the same table. — [BizBuySell industry valuation multiples](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/industry-valuation-multiples/)
- Food manufacturing: about 2.73x average earnings multiple. — [BizBuySell food manufacturing](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/food-manufacturing/)
- Alternative broker table: restaurants about 2.08x and manufacturing about 2.94x. — [Total Business Brokers](https://totalbusinessbrokers.com/valuation-multiples/)
- BIZCOMPS 2025: construction had the highest median SDE multiple at 2.14x. BIZCOMPS medians run lower than BizBuySell averages. — [BVResources](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/updated-bizcomps-data-show-whats-happening-in-the-main-street-market)
- Service businesses, healthcare, e-commerce, franchise resales: BizBuySell has a service benchmark page ([link](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/service-business/)), but no figures were extractable. A franchise resale guide exists ([VettedBiz](https://www.vettedbiz.com/resources/franchise-resales-buying-existing-unit)), but no figures were extracted.

**By price/size band**
- IBBA/M&A Source Market Pulse Q3 2025 (as summarized by a third party): about 2.0x SDE under $500K, about 2.5x for $500K to $1M, and about 3.0x for $1M to $2M. — [FISART](https://fisart.com/en/blog/ebitda-multiples). Another summary of the same quarter gives 2.0x / 2.8x / 3.3x. — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/26/business-valuation-multiples-by-industry-sde-ebitda-guide). These conflict, so verify against the IBBA original.
- IBBA Q2 2025: the sub-$500K segment edged up to 2.3x. — [PR Newswire, IBBA Q2 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ibba-and-ma-source-release-the-market-pulse-q2-2025-survey-302523561.html)
- IBBA Q1 2026: slight multiple increases in the $500K–$1M and $1M–$2M bands (no figures in the release). — [PR Newswire, Q1 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-market-pulse-survey-q1-2026-reports-the-latest-trends-in-business-sales-up-to-50m-302813653.html). The Q2 2026 release headlines only the $5M–$50M band (5.5 to 5.8x EBITDA). — [PR Newswire, Q2 2026](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-market-pulse-survey-q2-2026-reports-the-latest-trends-in-business-sales-up-to-50m-302858664.html)
- Asking multiples, from the Main Street Index (Oct 2026, 27,500+ US listings with price and SDE): median ask of 2.63x SDE, interquartile range of 1.73x to 3.72x. Asking multiples rise from about 2.35x at $50K to $100K SDE to about 3.92x above $1M SDE. — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/how-to-value-a-small-business)
- Niche example: express car washes at about 6.5x adjusted EBITDA. This is real-estate heavy and not comparable to SDE. — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/26/business-valuation-multiples-by-industry-sde-ebitda-guide)

### Inferences
- Rule-of-thumb screen for CA listings under $2M:
  - Under 2x SDE is cheap. It may signal risk or owner dependence, or simply a small business.
  - 2 to 3x SDE is normal.
  - Over 3.5x SDE needs justification: SDE above $500K, recurring revenue, a management layer, or included real estate.
- Asking multiples (median 2.63x) are about equal to closed averages (2.7x). Asking prices are higher, but sellers also tend to state lower SDE at ask than buyers later verify, or vice versa. Treat the comparison as approximate.
- Restaurants usually sell at 0.3 to 0.45x revenue. A CA restaurant listing priced at 1x revenue is a red flag unless it includes a valuable liquor license or real estate.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025/2026 sector multiples were found for healthcare practices, e-commerce, franchise resales, or specific CA service trades (HVAC, plumbing, landscaping). BizMiner was not reached.
- No California-specific multiples were found. CA multiples may differ from national figures because of higher rents and labor costs, but this is unverified.

## 4. Financing: SBA 7(a) terms in 2026 and seller financing

### Takeaway
SBA 7(a) remains the default for sub-$2M CA deals:
- Maximum loan is $5M.
- Rates are capped at about Prime + 3% on loans of $350K or more, roughly 9.75% at a 6.75% prime (Apr 2026).
- Terms are typically 10 years for acquisitions without real estate. This is standard SBA practice, not confirmed by a 2026 source here.

SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025) restored a 10% minimum equity injection. Seller notes can count toward the injection only if they are on full standby for the life of the loan. A newer SOP 50 10 8.1 (reported issued Aug 14, 2026, effective Oct 1, 2026) reportedly tightens debt service coverage, Quality of Earnings and equity-sourcing rules. That SOP is not verified against SBA's own text.

### Cited Findings
- 7(a) maximum loan is $5M, with no minimum, and business acquisition is an eligible use. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/loans/learn/sba-7a-loan); [sba7a.loans](https://sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loan-terms-rates-fees-maturity)
- Variable-rate maximums: Prime + 3.0% for loans above $350K (9.75% at 6.75% prime, Apr 2026). Loans of $250,001 to $350,000 cap at 11.25%. Fixed-rate cap for loans above $250K is about 11.75%. — [sba7a.loans rate page](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/what-is-the-sba-7a-loan-interest-rate/); [Merchant Maverick (Jul 2026)](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/sba-loan-rates/)
- Effective March 1, 2026, lenders may use the 5-year or 10-year Treasury or SOFR as alternative base rates. The rate still cannot exceed Prime plus the allowed spread. — [Orrick InfoBytes](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-02-12/small-business-administration-announces-alternative-base-rate-options-for-7a-loan-program/); [Federal Register 2026-02660](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-02660.pdf); [Starfield & Smith](https://starfieldsmith.com/2026/02/best-practices-new-sba-7a-alternative-base-rate-options-effective-march-1-2026/)
- SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025):
  - It replaced 50 10 7.1 and lowered the collateral-required threshold from $500K to $50K.
  - The 24-month seller-note standby option was removed. Seller notes used for equity must now be on standby for the full loan term.
  - The 10% equity injection is measured against total project cost (price plus working capital plus fees).
  - Sources: [Beancount SOP guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/29/sba-sop-50-10-8-collateral-equity-injection-rules-guide); [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions); [Angel Investors Network ("zero-down deals no longer exist")](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/sba-7a-acquisition-financing-rules-2026)
- SOP 50 10 8.1 (reported issued Aug 14, 2026, effective Oct 1, 2026): higher DSCR requirement, projections no longer counted, independent QoE required on larger deals, tighter equity sourcing. These claims come from a single secondary source. — [Beancount SOP 50 10 8.1 guide (Sep 2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/23/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-equity-dscr-qoe-guide)
- Lenders underwrite on tax returns and exclude claimed unreported cash. — [Arkansas SBTDC](https://asbtdc.org/buying-a-business-6-things-to-consider/)

### Inferences
- Example: a $1M CA deal plus about $100K in working capital and fees needs about $110K or more of buyer equity. A full-standby seller note can cover part of it. Debt service on a 10-year loan at about 9.75% runs roughly $15.7K per $100K borrowed per year, so a $1M 7(a) loan costs about $157K per year. At a 3x multiple, SDE of about $333K gives roughly 2.1x gross coverage before the owner's salary. Before the owner's salary is assumed, a business priced above about 3.5 to 4x SDE becomes hard to finance.
- Seller financing remains common in Main Street deals, but SOP 50 10 8 makes seller notes less flexible in SBA deals (full standby required to count as equity).

### Gaps
- The exact 2026 SBA guaranty fee schedule was not retrieved (FY2026 fees reset Oct 1).
- No primary SBA text for SOP 50 10 8.1 was retrieved.
- No 2025/2026 survey data was found on the share of deals with seller financing, or on typical seller-note percentage and term. A commonly cited broker norm is 10 to 30%, but no source was retrieved.

## 5. California-specific issues: wages, labor, bulk sale/escrow, tax clearances, leases, ABC licenses

### Takeaway
California deals carry extra cost and closing steps:
- Labor costs: $16.90 state minimum wage, $20 for chain fast food, higher local minimums.
- Mandatory tax-clearance holdbacks in escrow: CDTFA for sales tax, EDD for payroll tax.
- Successor liability survives even escrow compliance.
- ABC liquor-license transfers require a full-price escrow and ABC approval before funds release.
- Lease assignment is governed by Civil Code §1995 consent rules.

### Cited Findings
**Wages and labor**
- California minimum wage is $16.90/hr from Jan 1, 2026 (up 2.49%, CPI-indexed). — [CalChamber HRWatchdog](https://hrwatchdog.calchamber.com/?p=17820); [Employers Group](https://employers.org/2025/08/27/california-minimum-wage-announced-for-2026/)
- Fast food: $20/hr for limited-service chains with more than 60 national locations, since Apr 1, 2024. The Fast Food Council has not announced increases, did not meet in 2025 and has had no chair since May 2025. — [CalChamber](https://p-hrcalifornia.calchamber.com/hr-library/pay-scheduling/wages-salaries/minimum-wage); [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/california-minimum-wage/)
- Health care workers: $18.63 to $24/hr depending on facility type, with scheduled step-ups. — [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/california-minimum-wage/)
- Local minimums are higher. West Hollywood's $20.25 is the highest in the state, and LA is debating $30 for some sectors. — [Piedmont Exedra/CalMatters](https://piedmontexedra.com/?p=1561759)

**Bulk sale, escrow, tax clearance, successor liability**
- CDTFA: the buyer must withhold enough of the purchase price to cover the seller's sales/use tax until the seller shows a receipt or CDTFA issues a Certificate of Tax Clearance. — [CDTFA Annotation 6812](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6812.html); [Stimmel Law](https://stimmel-law.com/articles/bulk-sales-law-sale-assets-california/)
- EDD: the buyer must escrow enough for unpaid UI/SDI contributions until EDD issues a clearance. Otherwise the buyer is personally liable (UI Code §1731). — [Stimmel Law](https://stimmel-law.com/articles/bulk-sales-law-sale-assets-california/); [Fennemore](https://www.fennemorelaw.com/buying-or-selling-a-struggling-business-aka-the-distressed-business-part-4-2/)
- *Schnyder v. State Board of Equalization* (2002): a buyer who escrowed the price but did not obtain clearance was still liable as successor. Successor liability is a remedy separate from the Bulk Sales Law. — [CDTFA court decisions](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutcd/sales-and-use-tax-court-decisions-s5.html)
- Clearance can take 60 or more days and may trigger an audit of the seller. — [Stimmel Law](https://stimmel-law.com/articles/bulk-sales-law-sale-assets-california/); see also [Beancount bulk-sale guide (Sep 2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/08/bulk-sale-notices-successor-liability-tax-clearance-asset-purchase-guide)

**ABC liquor licenses**
- B&P Code §24074: for a transfer with consideration, the parties must open an escrow with a neutral holder before filing, and the buyer must deposit the full purchase price. Payment is made only after ABC approves the transfer. — [B&P §24074](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_24074); [ABC application requirements](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/apply-for-a-new-license/license-application-requirements/)
- Within 30 days of applying, the buyer must file a sworn statement that the price has been deposited (§24074.3). — [FindLaw §24074.3](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-24074-3/)
- Retail transfers require a Notice of Intention to Transfer (ABC-227/227-A) certified by the County Recorder. — [ABC-227 instructions](https://www.abc.ca.gov/abc-227-instructions/)
- License price signal: CDTFA tax-seizure auctions of Type 47 (on-sale general) licenses in 2026 carry minimum bids of $18K (Lake County area) to $110K (San Diego). — [CDTFA notice](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/notice-575842.pdf); [CDTFA notice](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/Notice-655778.pdf)

**Lease assignment**
- Civil Code §1995.260: if a lease requires consent but states no standard (for leases on or after Sept 23, 1983), the landlord may not unreasonably withhold consent. A lease may expressly grant sole discretion (§1995.230/.250). — [Civ. Code §1995.260](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1995.260); [Bay Legal](https://baylegal.com/assigning-or-subletting-a-commercial-lease-in-california/)

### Inferences
- For CA restaurant and fast-food listings, buyers should re-cast SDE at current wage rates ($20 for chain franchise units, local minimums elsewhere). Historical SDE from before April 2024 overstates earnings.
- A liquor license can be a material share of a sub-$500K bar or restaurant price, and its transfer adds weeks to months to closing. Escrow funds are locked until ABC approves.
- Tax clearances (CDTFA and EDD) should be closing conditions. Buyers should also order FTB and a lien/UCC search, because successor liability can attach despite escrow.

### Gaps
- No current ABC transfer processing times were found. No source covered FTB (Franchise Tax Board) clearance specifics for asset buyers, or the Commercial Code §6101 bulk-sale notice (escrow Form) mechanics as applied in 2026.
- No source was retrieved on AB5 (worker classification) or PAGA exposure as an acquisition risk. These are relevant for CA service businesses using 1099 labor, but unsourced here.
- No source addressed treatment of personal guaranties on lease assignment.

## 6. Due-diligence red flags for small-business listings

### Takeaway
The most common problems are inflated or undocumented add-backs, claimed unreported cash, tax-compliance gaps, customer concentration and owner dependence. Reconcile SDE to tax returns and bank deposits, because lenders will only finance on the tax returns.

### Cited Findings
- Overstated add-backs inflate SDE and lead to overvaluation. Each adjustment should tie to the financial statements. — [BizBuySell CPA guide to add-backs](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/cpa-guide-sde-add-backs-business-sale/); [TruForte SDE guide](https://trufortebusinessgroup.com/understanding-sde-for-small-business-acquisitions/)
- Buy-side QoE providers commonly disallow 10 to 30% of seller-proposed add-backs. This comes from vendor content and is unverified. — (search-result claim; source vendor not confirmed)
- Claims of unreported cash are among the most common issues. Lenders use tax returns only and exclude cash. — [Arkansas SBTDC](https://asbtdc.org/buying-a-business-6-things-to-consider/)
- Warning signs: late or unfiled tax returns, outstanding tax obligations, payroll-tax and sales-tax issues, customer concentration, seller dependence, missing records. — [TruForte red flags](https://trufortebusinessgroup.com/common-red-flags-when-buying-a-business/); [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/small-business-due-diligence.md)
- An SBA pre-LOI screen should check the seller's financials for major red flags before the LOI is signed. — [ProjectionHub](https://www.projectionhub.com/post/pre-loi-due-diligence-for-an-sba-acquisition)

### Inferences
- Practical checks for CA buyers:
  - Compare 3 years of federal returns against CDTFA sales-tax filings (reported taxable sales) and bank deposits.
  - Compare payroll records against EDD DE 9/DE 9C filings.
  - Re-cast labor at 2026 CA wage rates.
  - Confirm that the lease term remaining is at least as long as the SBA loan term (commonly 10 years with options). This is a common lender requirement, but no source was retrieved here.
- A listing whose asking multiple only works if "cash not on the books" is included should be valued only on documented SDE.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data was found on how often listed SDE is restated in due diligence, or on deal failure rates in CA specifically.
