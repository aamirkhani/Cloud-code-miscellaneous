# Businesses for Sale Under $2M: San Diego County and the Inland Empire (Riverside and San Bernardino Counties)

**Retrieved: 2026-10-09.** Methodology caveat, important for the report writer: WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for every listing marketplace tried (bizbuysell.com, bizquest.com, businessbroker.net, bizben.com, dealstream.com, rejigg.com, sunbeltnetwork.com, a BizBuySell co-brand on nebba.com). So **no individual listing detail page could be opened.** Every figure below comes from WebSearch result extracts of marketplace **category or search pages**, run on 2026-10-09. As a result:
- The source URL is usually the category page the listing appeared on, not the listing's own page. The one exception is the BizBuySell multi-unit auto listing (#2458445), which is over the price cap anyway.
- "Cash flow" is the marketplace's generic "Cash Flow" field. On BizBuySell and BizQuest that field is normally seller's discretionary earnings (SDE). It is labeled "SDE (presumed)" unless the snippet said EBITDA or net profit.
- The original listing (posting) date was not visible for any listing. "Date seen" is 2026-10-09 for all of them. Listings may be stale or already sold.
- Year established and real estate status were mostly not visible. Listings were not marked as including real estate unless they said so.
- Snippet extraction can mis-pair a price with a cash flow figure. The search tool itself warned that "some price and cash flow figures are hard to match to specific listings." Treat each row as a lead to verify, not a confirmed fact.

## What specific businesses are for sale in San Diego and the Inland Empire under $2M, with full financials?

### Takeaway
I found 39 specific listings at or under $2M (21 in San Diego County, 8 in Riverside County, 10 in San Bernardino County). Most disclose price and cash flow. Only about 10 also showed gross revenue in the snippets I could access. Most listings fall in the $0-$750K bands. Listings above $1.25M were scarce under the cap: the Japanese restaurant, the sign company, the Rialto linehaul runs and the Oceanside FedEx Ground routes.

Multiple = asking price / cash flow. ND = not disclosed or not visible in the accessible snippet. RE = real estate.

### Inferences
- Several low multiples, under 1.5x, belong to restaurants, bars and owner-operator shops. In these, the cash flow (SDE) includes the owner's wage. The effective return after hiring a manager is much lower.
- The Fallbrook shop's ratio, with cash flow at about 80% of the price, is unusually rich for auto repair. Verify it before relying on it.
- Owner financing (Riverside poke, San Bernardino logistics, Rancho Cucamonga salon) improves buyer economics beyond what the multiple alone shows.
- On a cash-flow-per-dollar basis, the 2-3x listings with all three figures disclosed may be the safest finds: SD plumbing, SD fire alarm, the Japanese restaurant, the Oceanside FedEx routes and the UPS Store.

### Gaps
- Trend data (multi-year revenue) and add-backs were not visible, so the quality of the SDE figures cannot be assessed.
- The SDE vs. EBITDA basis was explicit for only 1-2 listings.

## Any red flags visible (no financials disclosed, declining revenue, real estate inflating price)?

### Takeaway
The main red flags are:
- Missing financials on about a quarter of the listings.
- At least two cash-flow figures that are implausible relative to revenue.
- A few real-estate-inclusive deals whose price is driven by property rather than earnings.
