At 1.37x gross commission and about 12.8x weekly sales, the price is in line with other Routes R Us Mission listings. The true owner cash flow for a 4-route operation that needs hired drivers is likely well below $451K.

### Inferences
- **Not mislabeled revenue.** Revenue is shown separately ($2.5M), and $451K is exactly 18% of it. "Cash flow" here means the distributor's gross commission margin. That label is standard on Routes R Us listings: Napa's $210,246 is 17.5% of $1,201,408.
- **Price is in line with the broker's own convention.** $619K ÷ $48,225/wk ≈ 12.8x weekly sales, versus Napa's stated 13-1. Routes R Us appears to price at roughly 13x average weekly sales. Single-route comps from other brokers run about 1.0–1.5x stated net. Vallejo at 1.37x gross / 1.54x "net" sits within that band. The real question is the quality of the cash-flow figure, not the price multiple.
- **Labor is the big hidden cost.** One person cannot run four routes delivering $48K/week of product. Single routes with about $9–14K weekly sales take roughly 25–32 hours per week. A buyer would likely need 3–4 drivers. At an assumed $50–65K fully loaded each (my assumption, not sourced), that is $150–260K/yr, which would cut owner cash flow to roughly $150–250K before truck financing and route-loan debt service. On that basis the effective multiple is about 2.5–4x, still reasonable for DSD routes but very different from 1.4x.
- The single-route "nets" from the "Mission's Tortilla Route" listings (Encinitas, Kingsburg, Clovis, Murrieta) run about 13–14% of sales. That is consistent with an 18–20% commission minus about 4–6 points of fuel, insurance, stales and vehicle costs. Applying 13.5% to Vallejo's $2.5M gives about $340K before hired labor. This is a cross-check only.
- The $619K excludes truck purchases and four inventory deposits, so total cash to close will be materially higher.
