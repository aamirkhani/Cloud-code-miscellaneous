# Full-Service Car Wash Economics, Absentee Feasibility and California Regulatory Risk (Bay Area / Solano County, 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-10. Notes: bizbuysell.com and dir.ca.gov could not be fetched directly (DNS failure in the research environment), so figures from those domains come from search-result excerpts and should be re-checked on the live pages. Older data is labeled.

## 1. Industry economics: full-service vs express tunnel (revenue, labor, margins, memberships, valuation multiples, share shift)

### Takeaway
Full-service (hand-finish) washes are labor-heavy. Informal benchmarks put fully burdened labor at about 39-50% of sales, and closer to 50% for absentee-owned sites. Express tunnels run with roughly 3-7 staff and are the fastest-growing format. Small car washes sold on BizBuySell at about 4.1-4.7x SDE in 2024-2025. Growth in the industry is coming from subscription memberships, which favors the express model.

### Cited Findings
- **Market share.** Automatic tunnel/conveyor systems held 48.12% of the car wash market in 2025, and express-exterior is the fastest-growing segment — [Mordor Intelligence](https://mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/car-wash-market)
- **Conveyor revenue split.** Conveyor washes are about 48-50% of industry revenue. Within that, express exterior is about 24-25% and full-service/flex about 23-24% — [MMCG Invest, 2025 overview](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/u-s-car-wash-auto-detailing-industry-overview-2025). Other reports differ: one franchise-market report gives express tunnels about 58% of the franchise segment in 2025. Definitions of "tunnel" vary across reports, so the shares are not directly comparable.
- **Staffing.** Express sites can run with an average of 3-7 employees. Flex-serve and full-service sites need far more labor — [Professional Carwashing & Detailing, "Retrofitting for flex service"](https://www.carwash.com/retrofitting-flex-service/)
- **Labor % of sales (informal operator benchmarks, not audited).** Labor plus management often consumes 40-50% of sales, fully burdened. Another operator cites 39% of gross sales and says absentee owners usually run about 50% — [CarWashForum, "Ideal labor percent of sales"](https://carwashforum.com/threads/ideal-labor-percent-of-sales.12245); [CarWashForum, profit margin thread](https://www.carwashforum.com/threads/avg-profit-margin-from-each-car-wash.14113)
- **Margins.** One vendor overview puts full-service margins around 20-30% because of labor and customer amenities — [Epos Now](https://www.eposnow.com/au/resources/how-much-do-car-wash-owners-make). A 2026 blog citing "Car Wash Advisory" benchmarks gives 35-58% for full-service on an adjusted cash-flow basis. Those two numbers measure different things. The same blog says fixed costs take a much bigger share of revenue at about $800K gross than at $1.8M gross, a gap of 8-12 margin points — [Beancount blog, 2026](https://beancount.io/nl/blog/2026/08/13/car-wash-margins-cash-flow-2026-guide). This is a secondary source and should be treated cautiously.
- **Memberships.** ICA Pulse Reports: Q1 2025 showed record subscription growth, and in Q2 2025 subscription memberships kept visits strong despite weak consumer confidence. Q4 2024 showed home washing rising and price satisfaction falling among non-member (transactional) customers — [Professional Carwashing & Detailing, Q1 2025 Pulse](https://www.carwash.com/ica-q1-pulse-report/); [CW Daily News, Q2 2025 Pulse](https://cwdailynews-882f49.beehiiv.com/p/the-pulse-report-q2-2025-spending-holds-steady-as-confidence-wavers); [ICA Pulse Report summary](https://www.carwash.com/?p=67808)
- **Use of professional washes.** Drivers who mostly use a professional wash rose from 50% (1996) to 79% (2023), per ICA data cited by IBISWorld — [IBISWorld](https://img2.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/car-wash-auto-detailing/1695/)
- **Valuation (BizBuySell car wash benchmark, from search excerpt).** Average earnings (SDE) multiples: 5.96x (2021), 4.94x (2022), 4.58x (2023), 4.12x (2024), 4.73x (2025); 5-year average 4.99x. 2025 median sale price $700,000 — [BizBuySell valuation benchmarks: car washes](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/car-washes/). A secondary blog citing BizBuySell gives 4.93x for the latest year and an SDE range of 3.2-6.7x for single-site owner-operated washes. I could not verify that range — [Iconic](https://iconic.co/blog/car-wash-value/). Another guide gives 2.4-4.2x SDE for small self-serve and in-bay sites — [Beancount valuation guide 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/26/business-valuation-multiples-by-industry-sde-ebitda-guide). Larger express tunnels trade on adjusted EBITDA at about 5-8x, typically around 6.5x, per the same secondary sources. Those are EBITDA multiples and should not be applied to SDE.

### Inferences
- At a $300K price, the BizBuySell 2024-2025 multiples (about 4.1-4.7x SDE) imply SDE of roughly $64K-$73K. The deal is priced well below the 2025 median sale price of $700K, so it is a small site, most likely leased with older equipment. At 50% labor, a small dip in volume or a wage increase could wipe out most of that SDE.
- Express tunnels are capturing membership growth. A full-service site without a working unlimited plan, or without a path to a flex/express conversion, faces structural share pressure.
- Full-service sites priced below the median likely reflect lower margins, lease risk and owner labor. Buyers should check whether the seller's SDE includes unpaid owner labor that an absentee buyer would have to replace with paid staff.

### Gaps
- I found no audited 2024-2026 benchmark (ICA or Car Wash magazine) for full-service chemical %, utility % or revenue per car. ICA Pulse and study data are members-only.
- I found no source for the full-service vs express split of membership revenue.
- I found no time series showing full-service share falling. The decline is inferred from express growth and from older trade commentary.

## 2. California-specific labor law (Car Wash Worker Law, registration/bond, enforcement, minimum wage, tips, PAGA)

### Takeaway
California car wash employers must register annually with the Labor Commissioner and post a $150,000 surety bond, under Labor Code 2050 et seq. as amended by AB 1387 (2013). The Labor Commissioner actively cites car washes for wage theft, with six- and seven-figure citations. The state minimum wage is $16.90/hr in 2026, and I found no Fairfield-specific local minimum wage in 2026 lists. The 2024 PAGA reform lowers penalty exposure but does not remove it.

### Cited Findings
- **Bond.** AB 1387 (signed October 11, 2013) raised the car wash employer bond from $15,000 to $150,000. It exempts employers with a qualifying collective bargaining agreement and deleted the law's sunset date — [AB 1387 chaptered text](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/13-14/bill/asm/ab_1351-1400/ab_1387_bill_20131011_chaptered.htm). Older codifications show $15,000; for example, the 2009 Section 2055 text — [Justia 2009 Lab. Code 2054-2065](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2009/lab/2054-2065.html).
- **Bond terms.** The bond must come from a California-admitted surety, a copy goes to the Labor Commissioner, and it is payable to the "People of the State of California" for unpaid wages and related violations, including gratuities — [8 CCR 13682 (DIR)](https://dir.ca.gov/t8/13682.html); [SuretyOne summary](https://suretyone.com/blog/california-car-wash-surety-bond/). The Commissioner may not register or renew an employer until the bond and other conditions are met (Lab. Code 2055) — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2005/lab/2054-2065.html). The bond is continuous across registration periods unless the surety cancels it — [SuretyOne](https://suretyone.com/blog/california-car-wash-surety-bond/)
- **Enforcement, December 2024.** The Labor Commissioner issued about $1.3M in penalties and restitution against 19 car washes in the LA South Bay and Orange County, affecting 960 workers. The Commissioner said many car washes remain unregistered to avoid the law — [DIR News Release 2024-102](https://dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2024/2024-102.html)
- **Enforcement, 2022 examples.** Torrance Car Wash was cited over $800K for 35 workers — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2022/04/28/664866.htm). Shine N Brite was cited $908,998 after paying flat daily rates as low as $70 for 8-10 hour days — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2022/12/01/696885.htm). Classic Castle (Long Beach) citations were recovered through a lien on real property — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2022/10/05/688329.htm)
- **Enforcement, 2019.** Playa Vista Car Wash was cited about $2.36M, the largest car wash citation at the time. The president and general manager were named jointly and severally liable — [DIR 2019-37](https://dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2019/2019-37.html); [NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/playa-vista-car-wash-wage-theft/164357/)
- **Minimum wage.** The California minimum wage is $16.90/hr from January 1, 2026, up from $16.50 — [CalChamber HRWatchdog](https://hrwatchdog.calchamber.com/2025/12/california-local-minimum-wage-rates-increases-starting-january-1-2026/); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2025/12/california-minimum-wage-state-and-local-increases). Neither Fairfield nor Vallejo appears in the January or July 2026 lists of local minimum wages. Nearby Bay Area local rates as of July 1, 2026: Berkeley and San Francisco $19.61, Fremont $18.05, Alameda $17.76 — [DWT July 2026](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2026/06/california-local-minimum-wage-increases-july-2026). Local wage rules apply where the employee physically works — [Thompson Coburn](https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/is-your-payroll-keeping-up-californias-2026-minimum-wage-landscape-just-shifted-again/)
- **PAGA reform (SB 92 / AB 2288).** Effective July 2024. Plaintiffs must have personally suffered the violations they sue over. Derivative penalties under Lab. Code 201-204 and 226 can no longer be stacked. The $200 subsequent-violation penalty now applies only to malicious, fraudulent or oppressive conduct. Employers can now cure minimum wage, overtime, meal/rest break, wage statement and expense claims. There is a new early-resolution path for employers with fewer than 100 employees — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2024/06/california-enacts-first-significant-reforms-to-paga); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/employee-rights-labour-relations/1486586/california-enacts-first-significant-reforms-to-paga-in-more-than-a-decade); [Seyfarth](https://seyfarth.com/news-insights/paga-reform--ab-2288-and-sb-92-introduced.html)

### Inferences
- The $150K bond and current registration are basic due-diligence items. Ask for the registration certificate and bond, and check that the registration transfers or is re-obtained under the new entity before closing. An asset purchase by a new employer means a new registration.
- The citation pattern (flat day rates, unpaid overtime, tip handling) is the main inherited and continuing risk for a full-service wash. Individual owners and managers can be named personally (Playa Vista), and the state can collect through property liens (Classic Castle). Buyers should require 3-4 years of payroll, timecards and tip-distribution records, have them reviewed by a wage-and-hour attorney, and get seller indemnities with an escrow holdback.
- On tips: California Labor Code 351 generally treats gratuities as the employee's property. The bond covers gratuity violations under 8 CCR 13682. Management sharing in the tip pool is a classic violation. This statement relies on general knowledge of Labor Code 351; only the bond's gratuity coverage was confirmed in a source above.
- California's exempt-salary test is 2x the state minimum wage for full-time work. At $16.90 that works out to $70,304/yr in 2026 (16.90 x 2 x 2,080; my arithmetic). A salaried manager paid less than that must be treated as non-exempt and paid overtime.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the bill "AB 1974" in relation to car washes. Its subject and status are unknown and it should be verified on leginfo.
- I found no 2025-2026 Bay Area or Solano car wash citations; this search surfaced only Southern California cases.
- I could not confirm the current registration fee amount or the current statutory section text as of 2026, because dir.ca.gov was not reachable.
- I could not confirm whether Fairfield has a local minimum wage ordinance. It is absent from the published lists, which suggests it does not, but the city should be checked directly.

## 3. Water: reclamation, discharge permits, drought rules, utility cost trends (Fairfield / Solano)

### Takeaway
California Water Code 10950-10952 (AB 2230, 2012) requires in-bay and conveyor washes permitted and built after January 1, 2014 to recycle or use recycled water for at least 60% of wash and rinse water. Older sites are not covered by that requirement. Wastewater from a car wash in Fairfield goes to the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District under its pretreatment ordinance. Fairfield city water rates are scheduled to rise about 8% a year, roughly 47% over five years starting January 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Recycling requirement.** AB 2230 (Gatto, Ch. 545, Stats. 2012) added Water Code Part 2.12. Section 10951 requires conveyor and in-bay washes permitted and constructed after 1/1/2014 to reuse at least 60% of wash and rinse water, or to use recycled water for at least 60%. Self-serve washes are exempt (10952). Cities may impose stricter requirements — [Justia Water Code 10951](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-wat/division-6/part-2-12/section-10951); [FindLaw 10951](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/water-code/wat-sect-10951/); [AB 2230 enrolled](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/11-12/bill/asm/ab_2201-2250/ab_2230_bill_20120827_enrolled.html)
- **Water use per car (older, about 2012).** A study cited in the legislative analysis found self-serve washes use about 15 gal/vehicle, in-bay automatics 50-60, and conveyors 65.8-85.3 — [AB 2230 floor analysis](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/11-12/bill/asm/ab_2201-2250/ab_2230_cfa_20120820_202517_asm_floor.html)
- **Sewer district rules.** Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District Wastewater Discharge Ordinance No. 2017-01 covers pretreatment facilities, individual discharge permits, prohibited discharges and local limits — [FSSD Ordinance](https://www.fairfieldsuisunsewer.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Ord-1701-1.pdf). The treatment plant's NPDES permit is Order R2-2025-0028, adopted December 10, 2025 and effective February 1, 2026 to January 31, 2031 — [SF Bay Regional Water Board](https://water.waterboards.ca.gov/sanfranciscobay/board_decisions/adopted_orders/2025/R2-2025-0028.pdf)
- **Fairfield water rates.** Council approved increases on November 4, 2025, effective January 1, 2026. The 2025 rate study proposes about 8% a year, roughly 47% over five years. The residential daily fixed charge rises from $1.04 (2026) to $1.41 (2030) — [Daily Republic](https://www.dailyrepublic.com/news/fairfield-reminds-residents-water-rates-go-up-in-2026/article_b0e6ea63-94d5-4ce0-9ec3-84a33ca58765.html); [Daily Republic](https://www.dailyrepublic.com/news/new-year-new-water-rates-for-fairfield-homes-businesses/article_efab1bf8-4f02-434c-8ef2-ee679ec4e147.html). An earlier 2024 schedule listed the commercial base charge (first 2,000 gallons) at $38.83 for October 2025 to September 2026. That schedule may have been superseded — [Daily Republic](https://www.dailyrepublic.com/townnews/economics/council-could-approve-water-rate-increases-at-tuesdays-meeting/article_d8cf334e-6862-4c79-a1c1-25e65446e6f6.html)

### Inferences
- If the target site was permitted and built after 2014, confirm the reclaim system works, since a failed reclaim system is a compliance problem and a capex item. If it is older, it may have no reclaim system. That leaves it more exposed to water-rate increases and to any future city or drought-emergency rules.
- Water and sewer costs will likely rise faster than inflation in Fairfield through 2030. Model at least 8% a year on the water portion.
- Expect an FSSD discharge permit or registration, an oil/water separator or grit trap, and maintenance records. Ask the seller for permit, inspection and violation history. Stormwater rules mean no wash water may go to storm drains, including during hand-finishing and vacuuming in outdoor areas.

### Gaps
- I did not find current Solano County Water Agency or Fairfield drought-stage restrictions that apply specifically to car washes. Past California drought rules (2015, 2022) typically exempted or favored recirculating commercial washes, but this was not confirmed for 2026.
- I did not find FSSD car-wash-specific permit fees or requirements, or the 2026 commercial water/sewer rate table. These should be requested from the city and from FSSD at (707) 429-8930.
- I did not find PG&E commercial rate trends in this pass.

## 4. Equipment replacement cycles, capex, leases, environmental (Phase I)

### Takeaway
Hard published capex numbers are scarce. Operators report conveyor chain and rollers lasting about 200K cars, with yearly replacement at high-volume sites, conveyors depreciated over about 10 years, and repair costs climbing from about year 3. A buyer needs an equipment-age inventory and an independent equipment inspection.

### Cited Findings
- **Conveyor wear.** Conveyor chain and rollers last about 200K cars, and high-volume sites may need new chain and rollers every year — [CarWashForum, conveyor chain lifespan](https://www.carwashforum.com/threads/typical-lifespan-of-conveyor-chain.27115)
- **Conveyor replacement.** Conveyors reach a point where repairs exceed replacement cost. Stainless and aluminum components last longer. Chain, rollers or a full conveyor can be allocated over about 10 years — [CarWashForum](https://www.carwashforum.com/goto/post?id=147592); [CarWashForum](https://carwashforum.com/goto/post?id=147608)
- **Repairs and pumps.** Repairs "really jump up" in year 3 of an express wash. A high-pressure pump replacement can cost about 50% of a new pump stand — [CarWashForum](https://www.carwashforum.com/goto/post?id=207215)
- **SBA treatment.** Car washes are treated as special-purpose property under SBA rules, which drives appraisal and feasibility-study requirements — [MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/sba-car-wash-feasibility-study)

### Inferences
- For a $300K full-service wash, the purchase price likely buys mostly goodwill and used equipment. Budget a reserve for conveyor, pumps, dryers and vacuum overhaul. Without vendor quotes there is no reliable dollar figure.
- Lease review should cover remaining term and renewal options (ideally long enough to amortize the loan), landlord vs tenant responsibility for underground systems (reclaim tanks, clarifiers), restoration and removal obligations, and environmental indemnity.
- A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment matters even in a business-only deal. Many car washes are or were attached to gas stations, which raises underground storage tank (UST) issues, and they have clarifiers and sludge. Lenders often require environmental review, and lease restoration clauses can shift contamination costs to the tenant.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 published costs for tunnel, conveyor, dryer or controller replacement.
- I found no source on typical car wash lease terms (length, NNN, percentage rent).
- I found no Phase I cost or car-wash-specific environmental findings data. The guidance above is general due-diligence practice, not a cited source.

## 5. Absentee ownership: manager pay, theft/cash-control risks and controls

### Takeaway
Absentee operation of a full-service wash is possible but risky. The industry's own framing treats unattended in-bay and self-serve washes as the absentee-friendly format, and labor-intensive full-service as owner-intensive. Documented theft cases involve managers deleting POS sales, falsifying deposits and faking robberies. Bay Area car wash site managers are posted at about $50K-$83K plus commission.

### Cited Findings
- **Absentee-friendly formats.** Unattended automatic washes do not need full-time staff, which is why they suit absentee owners — [Good Men Project, camera systems for unmanned washes](https://goodmenproject.com/business-ethics-2/protecting-your-unmanned-car-wash-with-camera-systems-the-basics/)
- **Manager theft cases.**
  - A general manager with sole POS access deleted daily sales and pocketed the cash. More than $100K was stolen before the software vendor found it — [Professional Carwashing & Detailing, "Skimmer problem"](https://www.carwash.com/skimmer-problem-plagues-carwash-owner/)
  - A manager embezzled about $10K over a year by falsifying bank deposits — [PC&D](https://www.carwash.com/carwash-manager-embezzled-10000-one-year/)
  - One person embezzled over $234K — [PC&D](https://www.carwash.com/man-embezzled-234000-carwash/)
  - A manager faked the robbery of deposit bags — [PC&D](https://www.carwash.com/?p=45828)
  - Employees stole from customers' cars during full-service interior cleaning — [PC&D](https://www.carwash.com/carwash-employees-charged-theft/)
- **Controls.** Limit cash handling by using pay stations. Set POS security roles that restrict voids, discounts, price changes and pay-station cash. Do background checks and reference checks — [DRB, Preventing Employee Theft](https://drb.com/resources/learning_library/preventing_employee_theft_car_wash)
- **Manager pay (job postings, not a survey).** Fremont car wash site manager $49,920-$74,880. San Jose site manager $54,080-$83,200 plus quarterly sales commission — [EnergyHire posting, Fremont](https://dev.energyhire.com/job-board/car-wash-site-manager-3008-fremont-ca-6b84e8ff-057a-4f8c-82fb-890f3974be66); [EnergyHire posting, San Jose](https://dev.energyhire.com/job-board/car-wash-site-manager-3003-san-jose-ca-c39dc16d-dca5-4a31-96e4-daec0fa39589). San Diego car wash and detail assistant manager $68K-$70K — [CareerBuilder](https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/assistant-manager-car-wash-detailing-68k-to-70k-annual-salary-san-san-diego-ca--bade606e-345a-41d5-8aed-f252d55c4059). Lawndale, CA express wash manager $74K plus bonus (per search excerpt).

### Inferences
- A competent manager costs about $60K-$80K+ fully paid, before payroll taxes. That alone could consume most of the roughly $64K-$73K SDE implied for a $300K business (see section 1). An absentee buyer's real cash flow may be close to zero unless the seller's SDE already includes a paid manager. This is the single most important number to verify.
- A salaried manager must be paid at least $70,304 (2026) to be exempt under California rules (see section 2), which puts a floor under exempt manager pay.
- Minimum controls for an absentee owner:
  - cloud POS with role-based void and discount locks and daily remote reporting
  - cashless or pay-station entry
  - membership/RFID or LPR plans, which take cash out of the process
  - cameras covering the pay point and interiors
  - daily reconciliation of car counts (tunnel counter or controller) against tickets and deposits
  - owner-controlled bank deposits
  - a mystery-shopper and audit cadence
- Tip handling is both a theft vector and a legal one. Card tips must reach workers in full (see section 2).

### Gaps
- I found no Fairfield or Solano-specific manager pay data and no salary survey; only individual postings.
- I found no published data on the percentage of full-service washes successfully run absentee.

## 6. SBA financing (business-only, no real estate)

### Takeaway
An SBA 7(a) loan can finance a car wash business acquisition, including goodwill and equipment. Under SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025), a full change of ownership needs at least 10% equity injection on total project cost. A seller note on full standby may cover up to half of that. Goodwill is financed over at most 10 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Equity injection.** Minimum 10% of total project cost for a complete change of ownership. A seller note on full standby for the life of the loan can count for up to half of the 10% — [Grow America, June 2026](https://growamerica.org/2026/06/16/what-is-an-equity-injection-and-why-it-can-make-or-break-an-sba-7a-loan/); [SBA7a.loans](https://www.sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/new-sba-7a-loan-rules-aquisitions)
- **Current SOP.** SOP 50 10 8 took effect June 1, 2025. Under the earlier goodwill-based rules, deals with more than $500K of goodwill needed 25% equity and smaller ones needed 20% — [SBA7a.loans](https://sba7a.loans/sba-7a-loans-small-business-blog/new-sba-7a-loan-rules-acquisitions)
- **Goodwill and loan size.** Intangible assets, including goodwill, are financed for no more than 10 years. 7(a) loans go up to $5M — [CVBBA SBA fact sheet, July 2025](https://www.cvbba.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CVBBA-SBA-Fact-Sheet-v3-FINAL-JULY-2025.pdf); [MBBA 7(a) overview, April 2026](https://www.mbba.org/userfiles/files/7%28a%29%20Program%20Overview%20-%20MBBA%20April%202026.pdf)
- **Small-loan coverage rule.** From March 1, 2026, 7(a) small loans of $350K or less must show at least 1.10x debt service coverage — [MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/sba-car-wash-feasibility-study)
- **Lender case study.** One car wash 7(a) deal closed with a 15% contribution — [ReadyCapital car wash spotlight, February 2025](https://readycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/19978-RCL-Spotlight-Car-wash-PDF_v3.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative SBA math (not a quote).** For a $300K deal plus closing costs and working capital, total project cost is roughly $330K-$350K and the minimum equity is about $33K-$35K. A 10-year loan at roughly prime + 2.75% (about 10% all-in) would cost on the order of $50K+/yr in debt service. Against roughly $64K-$73K of SDE, before replacing the owner's labor with a paid manager, debt coverage could be near or below lender minimums for an absentee buyer. The interest rate is my assumption and is not sourced.
- Lenders will want 3 years of tax returns that support the SDE. Cash-heavy full-service washes often under-report, which caps how much a lender will finance.
- If the lease runs out before the loan term, lenders may require an extension or assignment as a condition of the loan.

### Gaps
- I did not read the SBA SOP text itself; the findings come from lender, broker and association summaries.
- I found no current published 7(a) rate caps or prevailing rates for October 2026.
