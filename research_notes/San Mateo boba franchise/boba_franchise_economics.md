# Boba / Bubble Tea Franchise Economics in California (2025-2026), for buying a resale franchise to run absentee

Research note (2026-10-12): WebFetch failed with DNS errors (ENOTFOUND) on dir.ca.gov, growthfactor.ai and fddexchange.com. So most brand figures below come from search-result summaries of franchise aggregator sites (franchiseoverview, franzy, fddexchange, vettedbiz, franchiseverdict), not from FDDs I read myself. Aggregators often lag behind FDD updates and contradict each other. Treat every brand figure as "to verify against the current FDD." The current FDD can be requested from the franchisor or pulled from CA DFPI, WI DFI or MN CARDS. I did not cover most of the long-tail brands on the list (Yi Fang, Coco, Chatime US, Boba Time, Teaspoon, Sunright, Machi Machi, Ding Tea, Presotea, Bambu, Feng Cha, Wushiland, 7 Leaves); see Gaps.

## 1. Brand-by-brand franchise terms (fees, Item 19, Item 20, transfer, litigation)

### Takeaway
Royalties for major boba brands run about 4% to 7% of sales, plus a marketing fund of 1% to 3%. Kung Fu Tea is the cheapest of the large brands (reported 4% + 2%). Tiger Sugar and Happy Lemon are the most expensive (7% royalty, with high initial fees reported). Item 19 disclosure is patchy, and I found no Item 19 figure I could verify against an FDD. Transfer fees run from a flat $10K (Sharetea, Happy Lemon) to 50% of the current initial fee (Tiger Sugar, about $42.5K).

### Cited Findings

- A consultant write-up citing the 2026 FDD Item 6 reports a 4% royalty and a 2% national ad fee, both on gross sales with no deductions. — [PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q14704)
- Reported initial franchise fee is $37,000 (one listing says $25K-$37K). Reported total initial investment is $169K-$378K. — [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/kung-fu-tea); [FranchisePayback](https://www.franchisepayback.com/franchise/kung-fu-tea)
- Item 19 is contested. One aggregator says there is no earnings claim. The PulseRevOps write-up says Item 19 shows a system-wide average AUV of about $485,612 at mature units, with the median nearer $400K, and that the average is skewed upward by NYC/NJ/Bay Area units. **I could not verify this against the FDD**, and the source is a consultant blog, not a primary source. — [PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q14704)

- Unit count is about 350-375 US locations. One comparison says 372 system-wide, another 375 franchised units, and local press (Jan 2026) says "more than 350 locations." — [GrowthFactor](https://www.growthfactor.ai/resources/blog/boba-tea-franchise); [Rock River Current, Jan 2026](https://www.rockrivercurrent.com/2026/01/rockford-kung-fu-tea-franchise-closes-as-owners-prepare-for-rebranding/)

**Gong Cha (Gong Cha USA)**

**Sharetea**

- **2024 FDD**: ongoing fees similar to 2019. Non-refundable fixed fees total $48K-$54K, covering the franchise fee, a technology transfer fee, an opening support fee, and a design/floor plan fee. — [FDD Exchange 2024](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/sharetea-2024/)
- VettedBiz lists a $12K franchise fee, a 6% royalty ($1,200/mo minimum), and **no Item 19**. — [VettedBiz](https://www.vettedbiz.com/sharetea-franchise-cost-and-fees/)

**Tiger Sugar**

**Happy Lemon**
- Royalty is 7% of gross revenue. The initial fee conflicts between sources ($30K vs $80K). ### Inferences
- On a $500K-sales store, the royalty plus marketing burden comes to roughly $30K/yr for Kung Fu Tea (6%), versus about $35-40K for Gong Cha or Sharetea (7-8%) and about $45-50K for Tiger Sugar or Happy Lemon (9-10%). That difference alone is roughly 3-4 points of net margin.
- Sharetea's $1,200/month minimum royalty penalizes low-volume stores (it is effectively more than 6% when sales are below $240K/yr).
- A 50%-of-initial-fee transfer fee (Tiger Sugar) is a meaningful deal cost for a resale. Flat $10K fees are more resale-friendly.

- Required suppliers and markups (Item 8), remodel requirements, term/renewal, and **owner-participation/absentee rules (Item 15)**: not found for any brand. These must be read in Items 8, 11, 15 and 17 of the FDD.
- Not covered: Yi Fang, Coco Fresh Tea, Boba Time, Teaspoon, Sunright, Machi Machi, Ding Tea, Presotea, Bambu, Feng Cha, Wushiland, 7 Leaves (7 Leaves is believed to be company-owned, but I did not verify this).

## 2. California wage law: does the $20 fast-food minimum wage (AB 1228) apply to boba franchises?

### Takeaway
Yes. A franchised boba shop in a brand with more than 60 establishments nationally is covered by the fast-food minimum wage, and DIR's position is that the type of food or beverage sold (including boba) does not matter. The rate is still $20.00/hr as of the latest sources. The Fast Food Council's proposed raise to $20.70 was never voted on, and the council has been dormant since around 2025. Kung Fu Tea (about 350+), Gong Cha (about 240+) and Sharetea (about 150+) clearly exceed 60 US units, so their California stores pay at least $20.

### Cited Findings
- AB 1228 defines a "national fast food chain" as limited-service restaurants with **more than 60 establishments nationally** that share a common brand or standardized features. Restaurants must be "primarily engaged" (more than 50% of gross income) in food or beverages for immediate consumption. Exemptions include certain bakeries and grocery-store restaurants. — [CA DFPI: 2024 legislative changes for franchisors](https://dfpi.ca.gov/legislative-changes-in-2024-for-franchisors/)
- DIR FAQ: the definition of fast food restaurant does not depend on the type of food or beverage. Coverage reporting says ice cream, coffee, **boba tea**, pretzel and donut shops can be covered if they meet the criteria. — [DIR Fast Food Minimum Wage FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Fast-Food-Minimum-Wage-FAQ.htm); [KTLA](https://ktla.com/news/california/californias-fast-food-minimum-wage-hike-takes-effect-monday-who-gets-raises-and-who-doesnt/amp/); [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/california-clarifies-fast-food-minimum-wage-law-taking-effect-april-1-governor-oks-stadium-airport-worker-exemption/)
- Contested point: some franchisees argued that snack/non-alcoholic beverage bars (NAICS 722515) fall outside the bill's NAICS 722513 reference. A union letter counters that the NAICS reference is illustrative. DIR's published position is that the beverage type does not matter. — [DIR AB1228 public comment letter](https://www.dir.ca.gov/AB1228/July-2024/Gabriela-Campbell-Letter.pdf); [SEIU letter](https://www.dir.ca.gov/AB1228/August-2024/Letter-CA-Fast-Food-Workers%20Union.pdf)
- The $20/hr rate took effect April 1, 2024. The council can raise it annually by the lesser of 3.5% or CPI-W. — [NPR](https://npr.org/2024/04/01/1241743207/california-fast-food-workers-20-minimum-wage-april); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/californias-fast-food-council-debate-another-minimum-wage-hike)
- A $20.70 proposal was considered in 2025 with a vote expected Apr/May 2025, but no increase was enacted. As of May 2026 the council had no chair and had not met in over a year. — [CalMatters, May 2026](https://calmatters.org/economy/2026/05/fast-food-council-no-chairperson/); [Restaurant Business: council dormant](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/californias-fast-food-council-has-fallen-dormant); [FirstHR](https://firsthr.app/blog/payroll/fast-food-minimum-wage)
- The 2026 general CA minimum wage is covered in [CalMatters Dec 2025](https://calmatters.org/economy/2025/12/what-is-california-new-minimum-wage/). The $16.90 figure comes from the assignment and was not re-verified here. Local minimums (e.g., San Mateo city) may be higher than the state rate. — [ECJ Law](https://www.ecjlaw.com/ecj-blog/california-minimum-wage-increases-for-2026-by-kelly-o-scott)

### Inferences
- For a buyer, nearly every "major brand" in this list will pay $20+/hr in California. Brands below 60 US units (likely some newer Chinese entrants, e.g., Chagee with about 9 US stores, Molly Tea with about 5) would pay only the state or local minimum. That is a structural labor-cost advantage of roughly 15% for small chains and independents, but it disappears once they pass 60 units.
- Unit count is tested nationally and includes franchised plus company stores (per the "set of establishments sharing a brand" definition). Verify the count at the time of purchase.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the DIR FAQ directly (DNS failure). Verify the current rate and any 2026 council action at dir.ca.gov/AB1228.
- San Mateo city/county local minimum wage for 2026: not researched.

## 3. Typical boba shop P&L (revenue, COGS, labor, rent, net margin)

### Takeaway
Industry benchmarks (mostly from vendor blogs, so low reliability) put COGS at 25-35%, labor at 25-35%, and a typical net margin around 15%, with a range of 3-20%. In California at $20/hr, labor will sit at the high end. After a 6-10% royalty plus marketing burden and Bay Area rents, an absentee franchise that pays a manager will likely net in the single digits unless sales are well above $500K.

### Cited Findings
- Toast: a 15% net margin is "typical," with a range of about 3%-20%. — [Toast, 2025](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/how-much-do-boba-shops-make)
- DojoBusiness: COGS 25-35% and labor 25-35% of revenue. Net margin is 15-25% urban and 10-18% suburban. Rent is $5-10K/month urban and $2-5K suburban. — [DojoBusiness](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/tool-revenue-boba-tea)
- Other margin discussions: [Menusifu, Apr 2026](https://menusifu.com/blog/bubble-tea-profit-margin); [7shifts](https://www.7shifts.com/blog/boba-shop-profitability/)
- A Santa Cruz County, CA listing claims COGS "under 11%." This is self-reported and an outlier. — [BizBen](https://m.bizben.com/business-for-sale/boba-shop-for-sale-in-santa-cruz-county-california-282256.php)

### Inferences
- Illustrative absentee California franchise at $500K sales: COGS about 28% plus proprietary-supply markup, labor about 30-35% at $20/hr plus a salaried manager, rent about 10-15%, royalty and marketing 6-10%, other costs (card fees, utilities, insurance, POS/tech) about 8-10%. That leaves roughly 0-10% net. This is my own estimate from the ranges above, not sourced data.

### Gaps
- No brand-specific or California-specific P&L found. Item 19s (if any) and seller tax returns are the only reliable sources.

## 4. Resale multiples (SDE) for boba/tea shops

### Takeaway
I found no published industry multiple. A small sample of live listings implies roughly 3x to 6x SDE for single stores. Short-period or inflated SDE claims are common.

### Cited Findings
- Garden Grove, CA boba shop: asking $180K, SDE $60,937 (about 3.0x). — [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/businesses-for-sale-in-garden-grove-ca)
- Franchise resale "priced for quick sale": asking $175K. SDE was about $30K for Sep 2024-Apr 2025 (8 months, roughly $45K annualized, about 3.9x). — [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/turnkey-boba-franchise-priced-for-quick-sale/BW2375745)
- Fremont, CA: $750K for a store plus Bay Area master-franchise rights, cash flow $52,612 (about 14x). This is not comparable to a single-unit resale. — [BizBen](https://m.bizben.com/business-for-sale/boba-shop-for-sale-in-fremont-california-286469.php)
- Other listings, with no price visible: Bossier City, LA (revenue $210K, SDE $55K) and Wichita, KS (revenue $633K, cash flow $220K). — [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/established-and-well-known-boba-shop/BW2533566/); [BizQuest](https://images.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/highly-profitable-boba-tea-shop-strong-margins-and-turnkey-opportunity/BW2476479)

### Inferences
- For an absentee buyer, SDE overstates returns because it assumes owner labor. Subtract a market-rate manager salary (about $55-75K in the Bay Area, unverified) before applying a multiple. Many small boba shops' "absentee" earnings may then be close to zero.

### Gaps
- No BizBuySell or broker-association multiple data specific to boba shops. Limited-service restaurants generally trade at about 2-3x SDE (common broker rule of thumb; not sourced here).

## 5. Halal considerations

### Takeaway
Tapioca pearls, nata de coco and most teas are generally plant-based. Gelatin risk sits in puddings, some jellies, popping-boba skins (sometimes) and some dessert syrups, and the ingredients vary by supplier, so labels must be checked. I found no US halal-certified boba franchise, and no source on whether franchisors allow substitute toppings.

### Cited Findings
- Popping boba and fruit jellies can contain gelatin. If the gelatin source is unconfirmed, assume it is non-halal. — [HalalFoodAI](https://halalfoodai.onrender.com/blog/is-boba-tea-halal)
- A Singapore shop: popping boba and jelly toppings sometimes use gelatin, which may be porcine. Halal certification there (MUIS) audits the whole supply chain. — [Froth Tea SG](https://www.frothtea.sg/?p=1187)
- MUI (Indonesia's halal authority) advises scrutinizing boba ingredients. — [Halal MUI](https://halalmui.org/boba-meticulous-halal/)
- Conflicting label example: an "agar boba" product is described as both gelatin and plant-derived. Verify the specific SKU. — [Walmart listing](https://www.walmart.com/ip/5343757741)

### Inferences
- Pudding (egg/custard pudding is often set with gelatin), some cheese-foam stabilizers and some jellies are the highest-risk items. Pudding and cheese foam are my own unverified inference. Franchise supply programs (Item 8) usually mandate proprietary toppings, so substitution would need franchisor approval.

### Gaps
- No US halal-certified boba chain found. No franchise policy on topping substitution found. Cheese foam composition was not sourced.

## 6. Industry trends 2024-2026: saturation, Chinese entrants, price pressure

### Takeaway
The number of US boba shops more than doubled from 2022 to 2024 (about 3.1K to 6.6K), and IBISWorld counts about 7.8K in 2025. Well-funded Chinese chains (HeyTea, Chagee, Mixue, Molly Tea, Lelecha) launched in California in 2025, adding competition aimed at the same young Asian-American customer base as Bay Area legacy brands.

### Cited Findings
- US bubble tea shops: 3,096 (2022) to 6,635 (2024), "expected to double again by 2028." — [Asia News Network / China Daily](https://asianews.network/chinas-new-style-tea-brands-find-a-hot-new-market-in-us/)
- IBISWorld: 7,845 US bubble tea shop businesses in 2025. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/bubble-tea-shops/6250/)
- HeyTea: about 30-35 US stores (Dec 2025-Jan 2026); a Cupertino store opened Aug 1, 2025. — [China Daily, Jan 2026](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202601/16/WS6969a663a310d6866eb341e5.html); [China Daily HK](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/627172)
- Chagee: 9 US locations, all in California (first at Westfield Century City, LA). It is targeting about 200 net new stores outside China, with the US a priority after a "tough 2025." — [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-finance/chagee-adjusts-after-a-tough-2025-with-big-goals-for-the-u-s-)
- Mixue: first US store opened in Hollywood, LA, on Dec 20, 2025. — [China Daily HK, Jan 2026](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/627647)
- Molly Tea: about 5 US stores by late 2025. Its Bay Area store reported $28K on opening day and $82K over its first 3 days (company claim). — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/chinas-new-style-tea-brands-find-a-hot-new-market-in-us/)
- Lelecha opened in NY and LA in July 2025. — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/chinas-new-style-tea-brands-find-a-hot-new-market-in-us/)

### Inferences
- Bay Area saturation, new high-design Chinese entrants, and Mixue's low-price model point to pressure on traffic and pricing for legacy franchise resales. This likely helps explain resale listings priced at about 3x SDE and closures and rebrandings of legacy units (e.g., Kung Fu Tea Rockford).

### Gaps
- No quantified price-war data for the US. Chinese-media store counts are not independently verified. No California-specific saturation data.

Tiger Sugar and Happy Lemon (7% royalty, high initial and transfer fees) are the heaviest. Whether absentee ownership is allowed must be confirmed in each FDD's Item 15.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- An absentee resale of a covered brand in San Mateo combines a $20 wage floor, a salaried manager, 6-10% in fees and Bay Area rent. Only high-volume units (about $600K+ in sales) are likely to produce meaningful absentee cash flow. This is my estimate, not sourced.
