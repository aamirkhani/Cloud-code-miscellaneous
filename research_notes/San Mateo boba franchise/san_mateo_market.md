# Market and Competitive Landscape: Boba Shops in Downtown San Mateo and the Nearby Peninsula (as of Oct 2026)

**Method note:** Desk research done 2026-10-12. Direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl) were blocked in this environment (DNS failure or proxy 403 for cityofsanmateo.org, yelp.com, bizquest.com). So every finding below comes from search-engine result snippets and summaries, not from reading the full pages. Before relying on any figure, especially Yelp ratings, listing financials and the minimum wage, check the source page directly. Older data is labeled.

## 1. Competitor count: boba and tea shops downtown and within about 2 miles

### Takeaway
Downtown San Mateo has a dense, saturated boba cluster. At least 8 to 10 tea or boba-dessert concepts sit within a few blocks of B St and 3rd/4th Ave, and most are Asian-imported franchise or chain brands (TP Tea, Molly Tea, Sharetea, Teaspoon, Meet Fresh, Tong Sui, Heeretea). Hillsdale (about 2 miles south) adds more, and every nearby Peninsula city has its own options. Any one franchise therefore faces heavy same-block competition. Leading newcomers such as Molly Tea (rated 4.7 to 4.8) appear to be drawing much of the demand.

## 2. Foot traffic and demographics

### Takeaway
San Mateo city is affluent (median household income about $153k), about 27% Asian, and has about 100 to 105k residents. Downtown foot traffic and Caltrain use have been rising strongly since the B Street pedestrian mall opened. Offsetting that: Central Park's main areas are under construction until about 2026 to 2027, a few anchor businesses have closed, and Peninsula office vacancy stays above 20%.

### Cited Findings
- Population: 105,661 (2020 Census). The July 1, 2024 estimate is 103,006, and another snapshot shows 101,327 for July 2023. — [Census QuickFacts](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/sanmateocitycalifornia,rosemontcdpcalifornia,hesperiacitycalifornia/PST045224); [QuickFacts alt](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/sanmateocitycalifornia,discoverybaycdpcalifornia/PST045224)

- Downtown foot traffic reportedly rose 20% between 2024 and 2025 and 120% since 2023. B Street has been a car-free pedestrian mall since late 2021. The article gives no underlying data source. — [The Almanac, May 21, 2026](https://www.almanacnews.com/ae/2026/05/21/renowned-restaurants-historic-architecture-and-a-car-free-corridor-heres-what-downtown-san-mateo-has-in-store/)

## 3. Retail rents, vacancy and redevelopment

### Takeaway
No reliable 2025 to 2026 downtown San Mateo retail rent or vacancy figure was found. The best proxies are boba-shop listings, which show $5,400 to $7,200 per month all-in for 1,400 to 1,600 sq ft. That works out to roughly $3.40 to $5.10 per sq ft per month, or about $41 to $61 per sq ft per year, which is broadly in line with LoopNet's roughly $47/sq ft/yr citywide average.

### Inferences
- For a boba unit with about $300k in yearly sales, occupancy cost of about $65k to $86k per year would be 22% to 29% of sales. That is high against the common restaurant rule of thumb of under about 10%, which makes lease terms (time remaining, options, escalators) a key diligence item.

## 4. Minimum wage, health permit transfer and local rules

### Takeaway
The City of San Mateo's 2026 minimum wage is reported as **$18.60/hr**, above the state's $16.90 and unincorporated San Mateo County's $17.95. It adjusts each January by CPI, capped at 3.5%. County health permits do **not** transfer: a buyer must file a "New Business or Change of Ownership" application and get written approval before operating. County foodware rules require fiber-based disposables and straws only on request.

### Cited Findings
- City of San Mateo minimum wage: **$18.60/hr effective Jan 1, 2026**. CPI adjustment capped at 3.5% with a catch-up provision. Applies to employees working 2 or more hours per week in the city. Tips do not count toward the wage. — [City of San Mateo Minimum Wage Ordinance](https://cityofsanmateo.org/3278); [City document](https://www.cityofsanmateo.org/DocumentCenter/View/99904) (figure from search summary; page not directly fetched)
- Unincorporated San Mateo County: $17.95 from Jan 1, 2026, and **$18.58 from Jan 1, 2027** (bulletin dated about Oct 1, 2026). California state minimum: $16.90 in 2026. — [SMC press release, Sept 30, 2025](https://www.smcgov.org/ceo/news/minimum-wage-will-rise-1795-hour-beginning-new-years-day-unincorporated-san-mateo-county); [SMC 2027 bulletin](https://www.smcgov.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/2027%20Minimum%20Wage%20Bulletin_UPDATED.pdf)
- San Mateo County Environmental Health uses one "Application for New Business or Change of Ownership" (revised 12/17/2024). Office: 2000 Alameda de las Pulgas, Suite 100, San Mateo, tel. (650) 372-6200. Plan review requires 3 sets of plans, 1 set of equipment specs and a fee. Final approval to open must be granted in writing before opening. Environmental Health must be notified in writing of any change of ownership. The 2018 form version states that **health permits are non-transferable**. — [SMC Health food application (2024)](https://smchealth.org/sites/main/files/file-attachments/20241217_food_application.pdf); [SMC Health change-of-ownership application](https://WWW.SMCHEALTH.ORG/sites/main/files/file-attachments/application_for_new_business_or_change_of_ownership.pdf)
- County Disposable Food Service Ware Ordinance (adopted by 16 cities plus unincorporated areas): bans plastic disposables, including compostable plastic (PLA) and Styrofoam, and requires fiber-based foodware (PFAS-free in later guidance). Straws must be fiber-based and given only on request (also required by state AB 1276). Reusables for dine-in are encouraged in most cities and required only in Half Moon Bay, Pacifica and Daly City. — [SMC Sustainability foodware ordinance](https://sustainability.smcgov.org/waste-reduction/food-service-ware-ordinance); [2024 ordinance summary](https://smcsustainability.org/wp-content/uploads/SMC-Ordinance-Summary-2024-v-8.1.2024_EN.pdf); [Jurisdiction info](https://smcsustainability.org/waste-reduction/foodware/jurisdiction-information)

### Inferences
- With each January increase capped at 3.5%, the city's 2027 wage could reach at most about $19.25. That is an estimate, not an announced rate.
- The foodware rules matter for boba specifically. Standard PLA or plastic sealed cups and wide plastic boba straws may not comply if the City of San Mateo has adopted the model ordinance (not confirmed here). Compliant fiber straws and cups raise per-cup cost, and franchise-mandated packaging may conflict with local rules.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the City of San Mateo is one of the 16 adopting jurisdictions, or its effective date.
- Change-of-ownership fees and inspection timelines were not found. Call (650) 372-6200 or email EnvHealth@smcgov.org.
- The city's 2027 minimum wage announcement was not found. The city publishes it in the fall.

## 5. Delivery-app dependence and seasonality

### Takeaway
Delivery commissions range from about 15% to 30% of order subtotal depending on plan, which eats heavily into boba margins. No San Mateo-specific or credible U.S. data on boba delivery share or seasonal swings was found. General industry commentary says summer is the peak and winter is slow, with a spike around March when new flavors launch.

### Cited Findings
- DoorDash: delivery commission is 15% (Basic), 25% (Plus) or 30% (Premier) of subtotal. Pickup is 6%, and its own Online Ordering is commission-free. There are no monthly fees, and plans can be switched any time (article dated Oct 2025). — [DoorDash merchant pricing](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/products/pricing); [DoorDash commission explainer](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/learning-center/delivery-commission)
- Uber Eats: tiers launched as Lite 15%, Plus 25% and Premium 30%. Newer (2026) third-party sources report Lite at about 20%, pickup at 6% to 7%, plus about 2.5% + $0.29 processing, and a possible +5% Uber One surcharge on Plus. No official Uber page was found. — [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-segments/uber-eats-and-postmates-introduce-tiered-commission-pricing-for-operators-starting-at-15-); [UpMenu](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/uber-eats-commission-how-much-does-uber-eats-charge-restaurants); [Restolabs](https://restolabs.com/blog/uber-eats-commission-fee)
- Seasonality: in China-based transaction data, summer is the busiest season and winter relatively slow, with a spike in March as brands launch new flavors. — [MicroConnect Substack](https://microconnect.substack.com/p/vol-3-what-you-need-to-know-about)
- U.S. context: about 11,162 U.S. bubble tea stores (as of April 1, 2026, per Smartscrapers). The U.S. market was about $523M in 2025, projected at $560M for 2026. Boba is increasingly a standalone purchase. — [Food On Demand, June 2026](https://foodondemand.com/06032026/bobas-growing-influence-on-americans-ordering-habits/)
- Uber Eats has a dedicated Foster City bubble tea category page, which shows boba is actively merchandised on delivery apps locally. — [Uber Eats Foster City bubble tea](https://www.ubereats.com/category/foster-city-ca/bubble-tea)

### Inferences
- If 25% to 40% of a shop's sales came through apps at a 25% to 30% commission (an assumption, not data), delivery fees alone would take about 6% to 12% of total revenue. Buyers should request platform payout statements to see the real mix and plan tier.
- The Peninsula's mild climate probably softens the seasonal swing compared with colder markets. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- No credible U.S. or San Mateo data on boba delivery share or monthly seasonal sales was found. Several "average daily sales" pages appeared to be SEO spam and were excluded.
